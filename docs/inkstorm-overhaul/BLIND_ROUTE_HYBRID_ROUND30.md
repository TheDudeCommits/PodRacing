# Blind route image review — round 30 marker hybrid

Reviewed 2026-09-08. This is a fresh image-only comparison. No code, history, earlier reviews, browser, Blender, or runtime evidence was inspected. Each of the four concept images and each of the sixteen candidate images was viewed individually at full-frame scale, rather than as a contact sheet.

Candidate A: `output/gauntlet/round-30-light-before-markers/`

Candidate B: `output/gauntlet/round-30-marker-hybrid/`

Concepts: `docs/inkstorm-overhaul/concepts/02-salt-run.png`, `03-canyon.png`, `04-fork.png`, and `05-launch.png`.

## Verdict

**Prefer B for the general marker scale, with a lower-fork exception and a canyon crest tradeoff. Strict concept parity: FAIL for both candidates.** B improves the balance between the nearest cream-tipped posts and the environment. Distant post chains remain visible in the open route views. This is not an unqualified improvement to every cue: some middle-distance posts lose useful height against crests, vehicle silhouettes, and the overlapping fork structures.

Six pairs favor B, one has a material split between foreground scale and cue exposure, and the fork pair favors A for lower-route visibility. Those preferences concern the supplied stills only. They establish neither actual driveability nor FPS, temporal cue continuity, collision clearance, or behavior while moving.

## Strict comparison with the concepts

- **Salt run — FAIL in A and B.** Both share the warm desert palette and a visible route. The concept has a strong sweeping road ribbon, varied leaning rock silhouettes, fine surface direction, layered atmospheric distance, and an expansive authored composition. The actual view is flatter and more sparsely arranged, with smooth cliff masses and a small horizontal gate as its focal structure. Smaller posts improve balance but do not close those differences.
- **Canyon — FAIL in A and B.** Both provide a large natural opening, tall enclosure, and warm sky against cool shadows. The concept exposes a clear winding road through the arch into a layered, sunlit canyon. The actuals use much larger, comparatively uniform purple rock masses and sandy rises that conceal parts of the onward route. The surface detail, stratification, lighting transitions, and depth composition remain materially different.
- **Fork — FAIL in A and B.** The concept presents two readable road surfaces around a monumental central rock column, with compact cyan markers following the edges. The actual image presents an elevated constructed ramp, guardrails, and chevron boards, while the lower branch is substantially occluded. B's marker reduction does not repair the missing two-branch composition and weakens some visible lower-route cue height.
- **Launch — FAIL in A and B.** The concept opens into a deep, richly layered basin with a large integrated industrial settlement, many rock spires, and an obvious falling road surface. The actual launch and descent views contain a simpler cliff arrangement, a comparatively sparse valley, and a much smaller industrial silhouette. B improves foreground marker scale, but the environmental density, depth, road shaping, and industrial integration remain well below strict visual parity.

The supplied concepts also have a markedly richer, wider vehicle presentation and more integrated material treatment than these actuals. This review's route verdict does not depend on treating that craft difference as a route defect. No dedicated foundry-interior concept was supplied, so the foundry pair receives a relative marker judgment rather than an invented direct parity pass.

## Recommendation limited to this review

Keep B as the preferred general visual direction for the cream-tipped posts. Preserve stronger cue exposure where a lower branch sits behind a ramp or rail, and where a crest or engine silhouette hides a shortened middle-distance tip. The images support retaining B's restrained near scale while addressing those specific visibility cases; they do not support reverting all posts to A's oversized foreground appearance. Strict concept parity remains open independently of this marker decision.
