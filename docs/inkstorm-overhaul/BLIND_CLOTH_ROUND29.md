## What the target asks for

The target separates hard curved hangar shells, thin suspended cloth, intricate metal trusses, cables, machinery, and loose service clutter through shape and lighting as well as color. Cloth spans asymmetrically between visibly substantial poles, with thin overlapping edges, tension, folds, fading, and wear. The hangars have layered rims, overlapping panels, readable ribs, lit interiors, and packed working areas. Machinery and people establish a convincing scale. Repairs and damage appear integrated into assembled objects rather than applied uniformly across broad surfaces.

The candidate images share the orange/blue desert vocabulary and readable workshop functions. Their large simple surfaces, small sparse workers, regular layouts, and dark interiors remain much simpler than the reference. I am not penalizing the diagnostic camera for omitting the target's vehicle or its race-lane composition; the visible environmental assets themselves are below the target's finish.

## Pits — retain B

**Pit 1:** A's blue awning has a continuous stacked blue/orange edge that resembles trim on a bent metal roof. B removes that strong rigid border and presents a thinner top silhouette with a broad drooping blue front. The light replacement panel at the right end breaks the uniform surface and makes the canopy look maintained from available pieces. This improves separation from the rigid arched hangar behind it. The right orange canopy receives the same benefit. However, the blue canopy's large straight fold breaks and the angular cream corner still resemble folded sheet material. Neither version shows convincing small folds gathering toward fasteners.

**Pit 2:** The open sky behind the awnings makes the difference easier to read. B's tapering left tip and untrimmed orange canopy look lighter than A's extruded-looking continuous rim. The material distinction persists in this lighting. B's cream panel remains a high-contrast geometric wedge with little visible seam logic; it reads as a replacement panel but not yet specifically as a sewn canvas repair. The center sag still consists of only a few broad planar changes.

**Pit 3:** Stronger illumination makes A's double rim look particularly solid and manufactured. B is the better fabric candidate here too, but its broad blue face and the sharply cut cream corner make the faceting conspicuous. The same large hangar shell, evenly placed service gantry, blocklike crates, and small staff remain visible in both. B does not improve that underlying construction or the substantial empty volume inside the bay.

Across all three pits, the arch silhouette and the readable engine-service arrangement are preserved. I see no new lost prop or major silhouette obstruction outside the awnings. The visible cost is reduced edge definition in shadow and a conspicuous geometric light patch. Those costs are smaller than the gain from removing the rigid awning trim. The gain should be described as improved material differentiation, not completed cloth realism.

## Districts — retain B

**District 1:** B's rust-colored central awning clearly separates from the blue walls and steel-looking catwalk. Its dark rectangular patch and differently colored end pieces introduce an obvious repair story that A's nearly uniform dark blue underside lacks. The left neighboring blue awning also shows a contrasting repair area. The thinner edge helps distinguish cloth from the flat roofs behind it. The central canopy still has large triangular planar transitions, and its attachments are not described well enough to explain its folds. The lower prop arrangement and sparsely stocked wall racks are essentially unchanged.

**District 2:** In the brighter view against orange rock, B's central patch and weathering remain readable, and the fabric is easier to identify as a separate material than A's dark framed band. A's parallel edge stripes again resemble fabricated roof trim. B's pale perimeter could plausibly be a hem, but its regular thin line and sharp peaks still need more physical explanation. A large dune visibly intrudes through the center-left workshop area and obscures part of the floor and equipment supports in both versions. That is a substantial settlement-believability defect, independent of the cloth improvement.

**District 3:** This view has a more readable central floor, so the full service display is easier to judge. B preserves that readability while adding a warmer cloth layer over the blue wall. The patch placement is visible, but still very flat and rectangular. The center canopy appears pulled into a handful of stiff facets rather than many small tension folds. Repeated racks, boxes, straight structural members, and tiny figures remain too schematic for the target's richly assembled bazaar/workshop appearance.

**District 4:** B repeats its warmer material separation and clearer repair language under this sky background. The foreground sand ridge hides the bottom of the cart and much of the standing area, while the left support appears to emerge directly from sand. Both versions consequently read as a partially buried display rather than a clearly functioning workshop frontage. The cloth change does not fix this. B is still the better awning, but this screenshot cannot support a claim of believable finished settlement staging.

Across the district family, B's color and patches carry more of the improvement than its fold shape does. The broad red surface is an effective color break, though it can flatten into the red surroundings in warmer lighting. The silhouette remains recognizable and does not obscure important machinery relative to A. No broader construction/detail upgrade is visible in this A/B comparison.

## Strongest next action

**For this cloth pass: author one canopy whose fold pattern visibly follows its attachment points.** Keep B's thin-edge direction and restrained blue/rust palette. Add a few legible tied corners or eyelets, folds that converge at those points, irregular shallow sag between them, a real hem, and small replacement pieces whose seams follow the curved surface. Reduce the broad straight-edged wedges, especially the cream pit corner. Verify the result at these same views before propagating it. More generic surface dirt alone will not solve the remaining material ambiguity.

For overall target parity, the larger next step is a fully staged and grounded workshop bay: correctly seated floor/supports, overlapping shell panels and ribs, enough human-scale equipment and purposeful clutter to occupy the space, and local lighting that reveals the work area. Resolve the obvious district sand intrusion before judging that frontage as finished. The target's depth comes from functional layers at several scales; changing the canopy material cannot substitute for those layers.

**Acceptance boundary:** B is the preferred incremental still-image result in both families. Neither version receives target-art parity, motion, performance, gameplay, or production acceptance from this review.
