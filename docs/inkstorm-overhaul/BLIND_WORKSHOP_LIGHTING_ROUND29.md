## Glare, leakage, and color

- No obvious new glare, clipped white hotspot, bloom veil, or washed-out cyan/orange color is visible in B. Its small amber accents preserve the palette.
- No clear new light leaking through a wall or canopy can be identified from these images. This is an image-level observation, not proof of correct occlusion.
- Warmth stays restrained, but its locality is not strongly explained by illumination falling onto a work surface. A visible warm lamp sliver is clearer than its practical effect on the work area.
- The bright diagonal streaks across foreground and workshop edges are present in both versions. They visually cut across structures and distract from construction reading; the comparison does not establish their cause.

## Unchanged parity blockers

The target combines warm localized workshop lamps, deep but varied recesses, worn metal, stretched cloth, dense functional equipment, and convincing human scale. Here, the large violet rear surfaces remain broad and shallow in tone. Cyan shells, tool cabinets, cloth-like canopies, and structural members mostly separate by paint color rather than distinct surface response. Engine assemblies remain simple cylinders and radial faces, and the sparse, repeated props do not yet suggest the target's busy working settlement.

Construction remains a separate problem. Canopies have broad rigid-looking spans and repeated pole arrangements; the walkways and facade connections lack the target's dense mechanical joining detail. In district-2, sand visibly rises into the central work area around equipment. In district-4, a foreground dune obscures the lower workers and tools. These terrain/building relationships weaken the impression of a deliberately usable workshop. Lighting does not resolve them.

**Retain B as a modest lighting improvement only. Neither set is convincingly near the target, and the visible evidence does not support passing environmental or workshop parity.**
