# Static scenery lighting

7 September 2026, local work. The world now casts directional shadows from its actual authored meshes onto sandstone, machinery, the racing surface, analytic terrain and moving craft. Emissive cores are added after shade so they remain readable under an arch. The sun direction matches the existing material and sky direction.

The renderer bakes all static placements, including objects behind the current camera, into a light-space RGBA depth atlas once per scenery revision. It fits the light camera to caster bounds and uses four depth comparisons with bilinear visibility filtering. Ordinary frames reuse the atlas and add no shadow geometry pass. This is scenery shadowing: the moving craft keeps its existing contact shadows, and the analytic terrain does not cast long-range terrain self-shadows into this atlas.

The initial resolution ceiling is 4096 for viewports at least 900 CSS pixels wide and 2048 for narrower viewports. A 4096 RGBA8 color attachment costs 64 MiB, plus its depth attachment; 2048 color costs 16 MiB, plus depth. Framebuffer completeness is checked before publishing the texture. Incomplete allocations retry at 2048 then 1024. A failed bake leaves the prior art-shadow fallback enabled and settles that revision rather than retrying every frame. Context restoration invalidates the atlas and triggers a fresh bake. The resolution ceiling is fixed at construction; it does not rebake on every adaptive quality change.

Caster clones share source geometry and materials; only temporary instance buffers are disposed after baking. Renderer target/clear state is restored in cleanup. Preparation failures and empty geometry also settle and clean up. Whole-frame draw counters reset before the bake, so a rebuild frame includes its submissions. Snapshot diagnostics expose atlas revision, size, caster batches, metres per texel, bake count and failure state.

Four focused lifecycle tests pass: course/context rebakes and receiver binding; complete-framebuffer fallback and failed publication; preparation failure and empty-instance cleanup; render failure with state restoration and source preservation. The first browser experiment (`output/gauntlet/shadow-preview`, build `index-B9JZ9aZi.js`) compiled and rendered all seven sections with zero page/console errors. It used a 4096 atlas at approximately 1.687 m per texel, with 15 caster batches. That experiment predates bilinear filtering, the final hero core, pit district, live junction framing and effects corrections; it is not acceptance of those later edits or sustained performance.
