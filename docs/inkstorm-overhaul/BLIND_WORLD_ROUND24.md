# Blind image acceptance — round 24

**Verdict: FAIL.** None of the seven world sections reaches the requested minimum of 8/10 in every category. The orange/lavender palette, recognizable landmarks and generally legible racer provide a coherent foundation, but the visible result is substantially below the supplied premium Inkstorm concepts in material finish, composition/depth and authored density. Sebulba is closer to its vehicle reference than the default craft is to the world-concept hero, but does not pass premium vehicle acceptance.

This is a fresh image-only review dated 2026-09-07. I read no source, implementation notes, handover, previous reviews or test receipts. I directly viewed the 24 images listed below using `view_image`; I did not open a browser or run rendering tools. Screenshots can establish appearance only. The displayed speed, race order, timer, airborne-looking poses and sequence filenames are not evidence of performance, physics, collision correctness or successful races.

### What the section images show

- **Grid:** The gantry frames a clear lane, but its large clean beams and empty shoulders dominate. The target has layered hangars, draped fabric, service equipment and small activity clusters embedded in a much richer settlement. Actual metal reads as flat color with scattered marks; the vehicle lacks the target's broad engine framing, fins, exposed mechanisms and cockpit presence.
- **Salt run:** The intended open desert reads immediately. However, large nearly empty sand planes, a few repeated small rock piles and broad cliff masses replace the target's successive sculpted spires and receding valley layers. The checkpoint bar and marker posts carry more visual interest than the landscape. The visible road is usable, but its long-distance continuation has little definition.
- **Canyon:** This is the strongest natural enclosure. The opening and warm sky create a useful focal point. The huge lavender rock surface is nevertheless too uniform, with repetitive thin strata and a sharply notched aperture. The target uses thick ledges, fractured boulders, rubble aprons and a deep view through the arch to a winding sunlit route. The actual crest shortens that view.
- **Fork:** An elevated left ramp and a lower lane are visible, but the junction reads as a road piece placed on sand. Raised lips, exposed dark undersides and tapered strips at its base weaken the connection. The target's central rock mass separates two routes with distinct elevations and clear continuity. Actual chevrons repeatedly point left without giving the two choices distinct visual identities. The banked default racer partially overlaps its own silhouette.
- **Launch:** The distant industrial landmark is recognizable. The approach and crest mainly expose sky, two broad mesas and a hidden road; the descent finally reveals a marked leftward lane. This falls far short of the target's expansive view into a layered basin, many independent rock formations and a richly developed settlement. Broad angular cliff tops, stretched-looking strata and sparse middle distance make the vista feel unfinished.
- **Foundry:** Cylinders and pipes give this section a distinct industrial vocabulary. Repeated tanks, open pipe ends and a start-like gantry remain too simple and regular. The target has overlapping horizontal pipe networks, bridges, cables, platforms, detailed bases and warm/cool practical lighting. A conspicuous faceted mound occupies the actual lane's focal area, with little readable route beyond it. This is an appearance observation, not a claim that it blocks driving.
- **Finish:** The barrier and chevrons clearly communicate a left turn. Their simple faces, repeated posts, clean rails and sparse roadside treatment lack the target's battered banked wall, framed industrial signs, dense rock shelves and distant gantry. The prominent default vehicle still looks much simpler than the target hero.

Vehicle material finish is 4.5/10, authored detail 4.5/10 and vehicle readability 7/10. A polished menu does not lift the vehicle or world to concept parity.

**Live drive:** Confirms the same appearance outside the seven named section frames: sparse dunes, a few repeated rock piles, large smooth cliff masses, conspicuously jagged distant outlines and a narrow, simple default craft. Its approximate scores are material 4.5, composition/depth 5, route clarity 7, density 3.5 and racer readability 7. FAIL.

**Fork approach and after entry:** Both show the elevated ramp separately from the lower turn, but neither supplies the target's strong central geological divider or equally obvious branch continuation. The entry's loose-looking roadway trim and exposed undersides are especially apparent. Thin disconnected vertical line segments are also visible in the upper-right sky in the fork captures. These are visible defects; the images do not establish their technical cause.

**Launch approach, crest and descent:** The descent improves route visibility to roughly 8/10 in that individual frame. It does not repair the vista: a single huge mesa fills the destination view and the lower route crosses an otherwise sparsely articulated basin. The earlier two frames do not communicate the target's depth or visual promise of a major drop. Still images cannot establish jump behavior.

**Sebulba concept versus live and garage angle 90:** The two large ribbed engines, orange/black panels, large vanes, long cables, trailing cockpit and visible helmeted pilot establish recognizable correspondence. Bright cyan exhaust forms read as simple faceted geometry. The live view compresses the engines to round shapes with diagonal vanes and leaves the pilot quite small. The garage screenshot also visibly says “Preparing Sebulba preview...” while showing the craft, which weakens the finished presentation; a still image cannot establish whether that text persists.

## Prioritized next actions

1. **Repair the visible fork integration and sky lines.** Give the elevated route a continuous, believable connection to the ground; remove loose-looking tapered trim and ambiguous road edges. Frame two visibly continuous routes around a substantial divider. Recapture approach, decision point and both branch entries.
2. **Rebuild the most prominent rock forms and launch vista.** Use layered silhouettes, real ledges, secondary spires, rubble transitions and distinct near/middle/far forms. Reduce the broad triangular cliff caps and repetitive fine striations. The launch must reveal a convincing basin and route below, with the industrial landmark embedded into that landscape.
3. **Bring one hero craft to the reference's finish at actual driving size.** The blue/orange world-concept craft needs a much stronger engine silhouette, exposed mechanical layers, fins, visible cockpit/pilot and crisp material separation. For Sebulba, preserve the already recognizable structure while improving orange paint, black machinery separation, edge definition and exhaust appearance. Validate the driving camera as well as the garage angle.
4. **Author section-specific density in deliberate clusters.** Add settlement layers and sails at the grid; sculpted spire rhythm along the salt run; rubble and ledges at the canyon; connected overhead infrastructure in the foundry; integrated banked barriers and framed signs at the finish. Avoid distributing more copies of the same small rock or post as a substitute.
5. **Stage light, materials and camera together.** Use stronger foreground contact, controlled warm/cool separation and atmospheric falloff to expose depth. Make wear follow edges, joints and usage. Preserve the currently legible lane and UI while increasing surface richness. The world-concept hero occupies a broader and more engaging foreground than the current default camera framing.

Acceptance should be rechecked against fresh images of all seven sections and the supplemental fork/launch views after those substantive changes. Small global color adjustments would not close the observed gap.
