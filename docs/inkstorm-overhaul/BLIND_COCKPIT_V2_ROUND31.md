Image-only review of A (`round-31-join-candidate`) and B (`round-31-final`). The primary evidence is each version's `01-grid.png`, `05-launch.png`, `04-fork.png`, and `garage.png`. Original targets are `concepts/01-grid.png`, `concepts/05-launch.png`, and `concepts/04-fork.png`. `concepts/09-open-cockpit-detail-round31.png` and the supplied v2 side image are supplementary construction evidence. The overall cockpit score weighs the model as it appears in the supplied race and garage images. The separate parity score weighs the entire supplied world view against its original target. A/B preference does not imply completion or target acceptance.

## What B improves

In grid and launch, A's cockpit ends in a conspicuous stack of rectangular plates above a thin projecting blade. Its rim, flat rear plate, and lower chassis read as separate assembled pieces. B replaces that fragmented rear silhouette with a continuous tapered blue shell. The cream rim also follows the opening more coherently. These improvements survive the oblique fork view and the smaller garage presentation, so they are not confined to one favorable angle.

The seated driver remains legible in B: the pale helmet, dark shoulders and arms are visible within an open cavity in grid, launch, and fork. I see no material loss of driver readability relative to A in those images. At garage size the helmet is still identifiable, although the driver and cockpit occupy too little of the panel to evaluate small details.

## Why finished-cockpit acceptance still fails

1. **The cowling's surface treatment does not match its neighbors.** In all three race images, B's rear shell is dominated by broad, relatively clean blue facets and a large pale tapered patch. The engines and exposed orange mechanical parts have finer wear, hardware, and surface breakup. A few panel lines and fasteners do not bridge that difference. The new shell reads as a simpler added cover on a more detailed machine. The pale patch may be shading or painted treatment; these images do not establish its technical cause.

2. **The rim-to-chassis relationship remains visually abrupt.** The fork view shows a smooth upper tub and large tapered shell alongside the orange, more intricate lower chassis, without a clearly authored transition of flanges, joining plates, or consistent trim. The supplied side context makes the concern easier to inspect: the straight lower edge of the dark tub sits above exposed curved machinery and a deep dark opening. This looks like an upper insert attached to a different body rather than a finished continuous enclosure. The race view already raises this concern; the isolated side image supports it rather than substituting for in-world acceptance.

3. **The cowling is disproportionately long and plain.** B removes A's exposed stacked construction, but its replacement reads as an extended pointed wedge. The construction target instead resolves a compact armored rear cap into a distinct narrow lower fin, with layered edging and wear that explain the silhouette. B's long unbroken taper loses that distinction. It is especially apparent in launch and fork; in grid its end reaches the image boundary.

4. **The cream rim is readable but still lacks the target's construction finish.** Its comparatively smooth, uniform band contrasts with the target's wider, chipped, mechanically articulated edge. From the supplied side context, the tall sidewall also hides almost all of the driver's torso. That does not invalidate the race-view visibility pass, but it limits a claim that the open cockpit is equally successful across angles.

There is no obvious catastrophic texture stretching or open crack established by the supplied race images. I would not claim a diagnosed UV fault from them. The visible blockers are broad surface shading, mismatched material detail, proportions, and insufficiently resolved joining regions.

## Original target parity remains separate and failed

Both versions retain a distant chase camera with a small cockpit and relatively narrow, elongated engines. The original targets put the driver, layered cockpit armor, and broad exposed engine assemblies prominently across the foreground. Their cockpit carries detailed blue/orange paint breakup and hardware; neither A nor B reaches that material richness or foreground presence.

The world views also remain substantially different in construction and density. The grid target has crowded working hangars, fabric canopies, and an intricate low gantry; the captured grid has sparse shelters and a large simple beam structure. The launch target has a deep, layered settlement and canyon vista; the captured view has much simpler industrial silhouettes and terrain staging. The fork target builds two routes around a dominant rock arch; the captured fork reads as an exposed ramp and a signed hairpin among detached rock formations. B's cockpit improvement does not close those gaps.

**Next bounded acceptance target:** preserve B's visible driver and coherent outer silhouette, then resolve the rim/tub/lower-chassis join, bring the cowling's wear and panel detail into the same material family as the engines and orange hardware, and distinguish a compact rear cap from the lower fin. Recheck the same grid, launch, fork, and garage views. Passing that cockpit gate would still not establish original world-target parity.
