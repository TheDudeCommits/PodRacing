# Galactic Racer reference study — round 36

Reviewed 9 September 2026. The reference is jackfrags’ [Star Wars Galactic Racer Gameplay and Impressions…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1FwcQlBZFP0), a 16:20.601 pre-release gameplay video. This is a different video from the OutsideXbox reference studied in round 35.

The most useful benchmark is the relationship between control, visible consequence, and readable road. A glancing collision costs speed while allowing forward travel; a stronger side contact can displace the racer into scenery and lead to a takedown. Surface changes produce a brief hover/camera compression followed by continued racing. Scenery gives the road a recognizable direction without filling its usable center with obstacles.

## What was actually inspected

Direct browser viewing at the selected 1080p60 quality, muted. Paused stepping advanced the visible player clock by approximately 1/60 second. Accepted evidence comprises **21 discrete timeline survey positions from 0:00 to 16:15**, **34 paused positions in four contiguous step windows**, and **26 screenshots sampled during three short periods of 1× playback**. Exact positions and exclusions are in [EVIDENCE.json](EVIDENCE.json). The four dense windows cover a wall glance, rival contact onset, descent, and landing compression; they are short windows, not complete frame coverage of those entire events.

**This is not an every-frame review of the whole 16:20 video, a continuous full-video viewing, or an audio review.** Moving-playback times were read just before the screenshot and are navigation labels, not independently decoded image PTS. Paused frames provide the finer temporal evidence. No remote video was downloaded, no paid analysis service was used, and no footage or artwork was imported into the game. Auto-generated captions were exported only as navigation text; they are not visual or physics evidence.

CUA displayed the inspected screenshots inline. Those calls did **not** save local PNGs, so there is no claimed frame-file inventory or image hash. A proposed two-frame file export was held by the parent for an exclusive game performance run. The owned reference tab was closed; no reference browser remains in use.

## Three implementation priorities

1. **Make collision severity consistent in the road frame.** A shallow wall glance should retain useful forward travel while losing speed and producing a local scrape; a strong normal impact or consequential secondary hit can cause displacement/damage. Validate the same wall geometry at several world headings. Compare actual ordinary-input clips, not only heading-invariant unit tests.
2. **Make terrain following settle, and handle edges explicitly.** Damp motion relative to the surface slope rather than interpreting normal uphill travel as a landing. A genuine landing should give one bounded compression/rebound and recover promptly. Abrupt terrain steps need a separate check: this reference study provides no justification for a step edge launching a craft upward or for hiding that response with the camera.
3. **Make the next usable road segment visible before adding detail.** Keep apex, exit and runoff coherent between collision surfaces and displayed markers. Use side barriers, broad surface contrast, spaced landmarks and advance corner cues where needed. Preserve room for a close rival to move laterally without an unavoidable obstacle; validate the actual first-time driving line at speed.

Official corroboration is separate from the frame evidence. Fuse’s [game-feel discussion](https://starwarsgalacticracer.com/news/feel-the-speed-game-feel-in-star-wars-galactic-racer/) describes intuitive vehicle analogues, high-speed repulsorcraft handling without a conventional tire traction model, and cause-and-effect as central to control feel. That supports studying physical response before adding visual decoration; it does not disclose constants or validate our implementation.

No source, asset, registry, gameplay, camera or test files were changed by this study. The reference provides design guidance, not source-asset permission or a claim that Inkstorm has reached its visual quality.
