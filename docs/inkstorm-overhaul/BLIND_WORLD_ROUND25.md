# Blind world art review — round 25

**Verdict: FAIL against the supplied Inkstorm target images. No section meets the required minimum of 8/10 in every category.** The actual captures establish a consistent sunset palette, a readable twin-engine craft, and recognizable section landmarks. They do not yet have the targets' premium material finish, composed depth, authored scene density, or racer presentation. The gap is substantial and is visible without consulting implementation details.

This was a fresh image-only review. I directly viewed all seven target PNGs in `docs/inkstorm-overhaul/concepts/` and all seventeen actual PNGs in `output/gauntlet/round-25/`: the seven matching section captures; `garage.png`; `live-drive.png`; `launch-approach.png`, `launch-crest.png`, `launch-descent.png`; `fork-approach.png`, `fork-after-entry.png`; and `canyon-arch-approach.png`, `canyon-arch-under.png`, `canyon-arch-beyond.png`. ## Section findings

**01 Grid.** The overhead gantry, canyon backdrop, side hangars, and blue/orange accents establish the intended setting. The target is a busy, inhabited pit lane: layered canopies, irregular workshop contents, grounded supports, weathered engineering, small figures, and a long procession of decreasing detail. The actual central gantry is dominated by flat rectangular beams and plain columns. The roadside shelters are sparse, and the low berms screen much of their usable detail. A large empty foreground separates the craft from the world. Add varied workshop clusters and convincing canopy structures, and compose their silhouettes at the race camera before multiplying props. Make the start gantry a distinctive finished asset rather than the same general structure seen in the foundry.

**02 Salt run.** The marked brown lane is easy to identify, but the target's geology funnels the eye into a deep, winding corridor through varied leaning spires and broken ledges. The actual consists of widely spaced landmarks around an open sand plateau; the largest left mesa has a smooth rounded wall with uniform fine striations. The low crossbar and evenly spaced markers become stronger visual events than the landscape. Introduce a staged sequence of nearer angular outcrops, mid-distance spires, and distant hazed silhouettes, with the road visibly threading through them. Preserve a clean lane while giving its edges purposeful geological structure.

**03 Canyon.** The large arch is present, and confinement is stronger than in the open sections. However, the actual arch opening looks like a jagged cut through a broad uniform purple wall. The target arch is a thick, layered natural bridge that frames a sunlit road and multiple distant turns. Uniform purple fill and very similar fine horizontal rock detail flatten the actual near and far surfaces. In the supplementary approach view, the arch is already substantially above the frame; the under and beyond views provide a narrow road corridor but little of the target's luminous reveal. Create a more convincing arch thickness and supporting geology, break up wall planes and color values, and expose a clear sunlit route through the opening earlier in the approach.

**04 Fork.** This is the weakest route composition. The target presents two unmistakable paths divided by an immense rock buttress: a broad lower route and a visibly elevated, winding alternative. The actual presents a freestanding metal ramp rising left beside a signposted hairpin. The approach and after-entry views do show both pieces, but do not make their relationship or destinations legible as the same deliberate split. Ramp edge strips and the pointed metal apron intersect the foreground's visual travel line, while the banking craft overlaps its left engine and hides much of the cockpit. Recompose the split around a strong physical divider, show both continuations before commitment, and resolve the ramp's approach surface and side trim into one visually clean junction. A thin segmented vertical line is visible in the sky at the upper right in the fork captures and needs removal or a clear world-space explanation.

**05 Launch.** The distant industrial skyline is recognizable, but the requested reward shot is missing. The target reveals an extensive valley with many height layers, eroded spires, dust-filled voids, connecting routes, and a substantial industrial destination. The actual launch and crest views are dominated by two rounded mesa blocks, sky, and a short road segment. The descent image reveals a bend and markers, but still little valley complexity. This cannot be brought to parity by atmospheric tint alone: the vista needs authored intermediate terrain and a more developed industrial silhouette. Frame the crest so the viewer can read the near drop, the landing/continuing road, the middle valley, and the destination in one composition.

**06 Foundry.** The dense cylinders, pipes, rails, and gantry are the strongest section identity in the set. They remain visibly primitive compared with the target: long flat-colored tank walls, repeated bright bands, open-ended pipes, sparse junction detail, and a reused gantry silhouette. The central sand rise cuts off the view into the next part of the route, while the target gives a long layered industrial passage with successive overhead structures. Finish a smaller number of hero tanks and pipe assemblies with designed seams, flanges, valves, supports, grime placement, recesses, and varied paint/metal response. Build overlapping depth and an identifiable next route opening instead of relying on sheer cylinder count.

**07 Finish.** The direction is clear because of the repeated chevrons and continuous outside rail. The target uses a banked road edge, convincing barrier modules, irregular rock buttresses, worn signs, a distant gantry, and a deep canyon drop. The actual is a sandy bend with generic signboards, an angular railing profile, a blocky trackside tower, and similar nearby/background rock treatment. Shape a more deliberate bank and outside drop, ground barriers into the terrain, and author a memorable finish approach landmark. Keep the functional chevron clarity while reducing the appearance of repeated identical boards on a simple fence.

## Shared visual blockers

1. **The hero racer does not carry the target's finish or camera presence.** The actual twin-engine silhouette is recognizable, with pleasing blue/orange contrast and visible connecting cables. However, the engine shells are broad faceted capsules with sparse detail; the rear cyan ends read as flat pale discs instead of layered turbines. The cockpit is a narrow enclosed mass, and the pilot is not discernible in the racing views. The target's wider low chase composition shows machinery, fins, turbine depth, cables, paint chips, and a clearly seated pilot. A different vehicle design can satisfy the brief, but it needs comparable authored richness and focal authority. Refine the engine silhouette and rear assemblies, add a readable pilot/cockpit focal point, and choose a camera that preserves the route while giving the craft a designed foreground role.

2. **Fine noise is substituting for material structure.** Rock has abundant thin striations, but large faces often read as smoothly extruded forms with a common purple shadow fill. Sand and track remain broad similar surfaces. Metal has some wear, but insufficient edge construction and surface-response variety to feel like finished machinery. Establish large geological planes and meaningful material zones first; then use medium fractures, localized deposits, paint loss, seams, and restrained small detail. More uniform noise will not close this gap.

3. **Near, middle, and far layers are insufficiently separated.** The actual has warm light and cool shadows, but nearby and distant rock frequently share very similar contrast and surface detail. The target uses stronger overlap, value grouping, atmospheric separation, and directionally arranged silhouettes. The open sections especially need middle-distance content that connects the road to the skyline.

4. **Track furniture is carrying too much of the art direction.** Repeated light posts, small rock stacks, low checkpoint bars, gantries, and chevrons identify the route, but expose the repetition. Create section-specific landmark families, cluster supporting detail intentionally, and allow geology and industrial architecture to define the track edges.

## Supplementary captures and artifacts

- **Garage:** The typography, cream/orange/navy palette, spacing, vehicle cards, and primary action form a coherent, fairly polished interface. The small hero render exposes the same plain engine capsules and does not create the premium vehicle showcase suggested by the world targets. No obvious major UI overlap or clipping is visible in this capture. Interface quality does not compensate for the world art gate.
- **Live drive:** Confirms the same broad empty sand foreground, limited middle distance, and simple vehicle presentation in an additional shown state. Rock and horizon outlines are visibly stair-stepped, and the overall world/craft image appears rougher at edges than the crisp HUD. This is an image-quality observation, not a performance conclusion.
- **Launch sequence:** The road disappears behind the crest, then becomes visible on descent. This supports only the visible change in terrain/camera composition. It does not establish an actual jump, flight arc, landing, or acceptable handling.
- **Fork sequence:** All three views retain the exposed metal-ramp presentation, unresolved-looking apron/trim junction, and thin segmented sky stroke. These should be fixed visually before another art acceptance pass. The screenshots cannot establish collision faults.
- **Arch sequence:** No gross missing mesh or catastrophic camera penetration is apparent. The principal issue is the near-uniform wall treatment and limited framed vista, not an absence of an arch.

There is no black frame, scene-wide missing geometry, or similarly catastrophic artifact visible in this set. Nevertheless, the fork sky stroke, prominent ramp seams/apron, and edge stair-stepping prevent an unqualified clean-image sign-off. The art gate fails independently because every section remains below 8 in several required categories.

## Prioritized next pass

1. **Rebuild the launch and fork compositions at the actual gameplay camera.** Use silhouette and depth planning before material polish: a multi-layer valley reveal for launch; two clearly separated route continuations around a strong divider for fork. These are major section-identity deficits.
2. **Finish one racer to the required hero standard.** Resolve rear turbines, layered mechanical shells, cockpit/pilot readability, material hierarchy, and chase-camera presentation. This improvement affects every section and the garage.
3. **Replace uniform terrain treatment with designed geological forms and depth layers.** Prioritize the canyon arch and open run; vary macro shapes, expose broken ledges and rubble at contact zones, and separate near/middle/far values without obscuring the lane.
4. **Build a small finished asset family for grid/foundry/finish.** Include convincing supports, edge details, grounded weathering, and a few distinctive hero structures. Dress scenes in intentional clusters with visible long-distance continuity.
5. **Remove the visible image defects and validate the same views again.** Clean the fork sky line and ramp junction, improve edge presentation, and check that no new dressing blocks route or racer readability. Submit the same seven section pairs plus approach/crest/under/after views for another independent image-only review.

The current set is an identifiable stylized desert-racing world with a coherent UI. It is not at the requested Inkstorm premium target parity. No pass is granted.
