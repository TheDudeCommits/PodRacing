## Fork

A: `output/gauntlet/round-31-fork-before`. B: `output/gauntlet/round-31-fork-candidate`.

Inspected both versions of `fork-325m-before`, `fork-275m-before`, `fork-225m-before`, `fork-175m-before`, `fork-100m-before`, `fork-45m-before`, `fork-35m-after`, `04-fork`, `fork-after-entry`, and `fork-downstream`.

- **Advance choice improves materially.** At 325 m the new board is a dark, barely readable object. By 275 m its two-branch diagram is intelligible, and at 225 m the cyan left branch and pale right branch are unmistakable. A presents a strongly left-directed corridor without comparably explicit advance notice of two alternatives. B earns a clear win here, without claiming its 325 m frame is already legible.
- **Directions and identities remain consistent.** The cyan branch on the left of the advance board corresponds to the raised route and its cyan marker. The pale branch corresponds to the lower route on the right. At 175–100 m the larger cyan diagonal-left and pale forward arrows remain compatible with those entrances. The repeated left chevrons describe the subsequent bend of the lower route; they do not by themselves reverse the branch assignment.
- **The lower continuation is more useful.** From 45 m before through 35 m after, the new pale double-bar markers distinguish the lower corridor from undifferentiated sand beside the ramp. In `fork-after-entry` and `fork-downstream`, these markers keep leading into the visible leftward continuation. They support a route that already existed visually in A rather than creating an apparent dead end. The ramp, engines, and terrain still hide portions of the immediate road, so this is an image-based continuity judgment only.
- **The added noise is tolerable, but the marker vocabulary is crowded.** B combines the new small route-identity posts with existing cones, large chevrons, arrows, rails, and the overhead frame. The small posts are useful enough to retain, although the lower corridor could communicate the same identity with fewer competing prop types. They read as race infrastructure rather than unrelated scenery.
- **The foundation remains visibly unfinished relative to the target.** The raised route is still a smooth ribbon with long planar side faces and abrupt edges against the sand. Repeated large rock shapes and sparse ground treatment remain conspicuous. The target's massive central rock divide, naturally embedded two-route geometry, irregular shoulders, and atmospheric depth are not achieved by the signage improvement.

Retain B. Its bounded guidance task passes in these samples. Do not interpret that as approval of the fork's final scenery or strict visual parity.

## Cockpit

A: `output/gauntlet/round-31-fork-candidate`. B: `output/gauntlet/round-31-cockpit-candidate`.

Inspected both versions of `01-grid`, `05-launch`, `04-fork`, and `garage`; also inspected B's `04-fork` at original resolution.

- **Driver visibility clearly improves.** A's tall central cover largely conceals the occupant from the race camera. B exposes a recognizable helmet, shoulders, and seated upper body in all three race views. In the garage the opening is evident, although the driver is too small there to judge fine character finish.
- **Forward visibility is preserved and slightly improved.** B reduces the central obstruction below and between the engines. The lower cockpit does not cover the branch entrances, launch landing view, or HUD. This part of the bounded task passes.
- **The opening is intentional, but the assembly does not yet look fully finished.** The continuous pale rim and dark interior establish a purposeful open cockpit. I do not see a clearly detached rim or an obvious hole through the seating area. However, the flat rectangular projections at the two ends, abrupt transitions from the tub into the large side chassis, and exposed stacked lower pieces give the assembly a cut-down, pieced-together silhouette. These are visible in the grid and launch views and particularly clear at the oblique fork angle. The continuous rim alone does not resolve those transitions.
- **Style cohesion is partial.** The dark trim, pale edging, and worn blue/brown materials broadly belong to the existing vehicle. The small helmet and plain tub still lack enough shaped bodywork and material hierarchy to make the pilot compartment feel as authored as the larger engines. B is preferable to hiding the pilot, but the requested finished cockpit package remains short of acceptance.
- **Strict parity is a large separate gap.** All three targets show a much broader foreground cockpit, a strongly staged pilot, layered sculpted bodywork, and integrated mechanical detail. The candidate remains distant and narrow in frame, with a small helmet in a basic open tub. Neither A nor B matches the target's vehicle presence, detailing, composition, or finish.

Retain B as the better base because it makes the driver readable and establishes a coherent opening. The bounded finished-cockpit verdict remains FAIL until the end blocks, rim-to-body transitions, and exposed lower silhouette resolve into one deliberate vehicle form. Strict parity also remains FAIL.
