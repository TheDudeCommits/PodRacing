# Blind visual review — round 4

**Overall correspondence to the supplied Inkstorm targets: 3/10. Visual acceptance: fail.** The warm desert palette, cyan twin engines, trailing central craft, and broadly readable road carry over. The targets' authored geology, inhabited industrial setting, dramatic depth, painted material hierarchy, and low racing posture do not yet carry over convincingly. These frames read as a stylized racing prototype, well below the requested painterly AAA spectacle.

This review used only the seven numbered PNGs in `output/gauntlet/round-4/` and their same-named concept PNGs in `docs/inkstorm-overhaul/concepts/`. No source, implementation reports, earlier critiques, or receipts were consulted. All observations concern what is visible in these captures. A landmark absent from a capture might exist elsewhere; it still does not fulfill the captured comparison. Frame rate, animation, controls, collision, actual speed, and whether 40–60 fps is maintained cannot be judged from stills.

## Cross-scene gaps

**World depth and geological authorship — 3/10.** In `02-salt-run`, `04-fork`, `05-launch`, and `07-finish`, narrow pillar silhouettes repeat with similar widths, upright poses, and forked tops. Their size does not translate into believable geological scale because the ground lacks intermediate debris fields, broken ledges, terraces, and layered distance. The target scenes have a hierarchy of one or two memorable masses, supporting formations, and small scale cues. Repetition is currently the subject of the image. Author the large masses and vista compositions first; additional repetitions will not close this gap.

**Materials and painterly treatment — 3/10.** The rocks carry a detailed horizontal sediment pattern, while the track and dunes are broad cream-colored polygons with soft, regular longitudinal bands. This mismatch is especially visible where the left wall meets the road in `03-canyon`. The target uses angular painted value planes at large scale, broken chips at medium scale, and controlled fine wear. Ground needs warm rust/ochre variation, scuffed racing lines, irregular grit, and convincing transition into rock bases. Architecture needs darker worn metal surfaces rather than the nearly white bands dominating `01-grid` and `06-foundry`. Texture density alone is not painterly coherence.

**Lighting and atmosphere — 3/10.** The current frames share pale, broadly even illumination. Some cast shadows exist, but the large rock walls remain similarly readable on most faces, and distant formations retain too much local contrast. The white circular sun in `02-salt-run` and `04-fork` looks disconnected from the broad sky glow. The target uses blue-violet shadow masses, sharp warm sun planes, dark crevices, and distinct foreground-to-distance separation. `03-canyon` particularly needs its foreground and arch underside to fall into deep cool shadow while the route ahead remains sunlit. Preserve road readability through value composition, not uniform brightness.

**Craft — 5/10; pilot — 2.5/10.** The current twin-engine shape, cyan rings, navy/cyan/red color blocks, and some wear are recognizable and stronger than the world. The engines still read as fairly simple tubes with clean rings, unlike the target's layered housings, exposed mechanics, chipped fins, and curved tow cables. Across all seven captures, the pilot has a tall upright geometric torso, a simple round helmet, and widely extended straight arms. The target pilot is a small hunched figure nested deeply in a narrow cockpit. Lower the seated torso into the shell, bend the arms into reachable controls, shape the helmet and shoulders, and wrap the cockpit around the body. Move the engine composition outward and lower the pilot's silhouette so the world and speed line dominate rather than the mannequin.

**Visual promise of speed — 3.5/10.** Sparse edge streaks and the HUD's 410 KPH number imply motion, but the large pale ground polygons, distant isolated props, high upright rider, and limited near-road detail do little to sell racing velocity in a still. The target uses low framing, strong converging surface marks, close lateral scenery, engine thrust, and long moving-ground accents. Add near-ground texture and roadside scale cues, then tune restrained directional effects. This is a still-image assessment of the speed impression, not a judgment of actual handling or animation.

**Readability — 6/10.** The central driving space and large HUD numbers are readable. The HUD and bright green posts separate clearly from the environment. However, those posts look like generic luminous markers, and the fork/launch/foundry identities rely on labels because their distinct geography is not visible. The empty race-order panel in each capture uses valuable screen area without adding a visible field of opponents. Hazard panels are legible, but in `03-canyon` and `05-launch` the actual hazard is not comparably obvious from the frame. Give each hazard a distinct world silhouette/material and preserve a clear association between it and its alert.

## Priority order and visible acceptance checks

1. **Make each scene recognizable without its filename or HUD.** First build the natural canyon arch (`03`), split elevated fork (`04`), basin-and-foundry launch reveal (`05`), dense pipe/tank passage (`06`), and banked cliff finish (`07`). These are large compositional differences, not polishing issues. A blind viewer should be able to match the seven scenes to the concepts by geometry alone.
2. **Replace the repeated pillar landscape with composed terrain layers.** `02` and `05` should each have a unique foreground mass, visible road at middle distance, and at least two progressively softer distant terrain layers. Vary silhouette, inclination, breakup, and clustered bases; avoid evenly scattered totems.
3. **Unify ground, rock, and architecture under the target's value structure.** Compare `03` with its target in grayscale: the target has a strong dark enclosure and luminous exit; the current frame does not. Warm and break up the road, deepen occlusion, shape sunlit planes, and haze distant terrain before adding fine detail.
4. **Rebuild the seated silhouette and cockpit framing visible in every shot.** The pilot should appear safely inside a powerful racing machine, leaning toward controls, with a much smaller upper-body silhouette. Preserve the already legible twin engines while adding a few large mechanical layers and believable curved connections.
5. **Resolve visual ambiguity and finish the race venue dressing.** The yellow geometry in `04` and `07`, bright generic gate in `05`, and nearly white architecture in `01`/`06` currently compete with the intended world. Give them coherent identities and materials. Add spectators/workshop scale cues at the grid, chevrons/barriers at the finish, and readable in-world hazards.

The 40–60 fps goal should be tested independently after these changes using actual playback and recorded frame timings. These stills do not justify an exception to the visual target, nor establish whether the current renderer has room for additional detail.
