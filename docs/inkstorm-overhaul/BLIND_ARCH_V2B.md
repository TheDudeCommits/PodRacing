# Blind arch V2B geometry review

Date: 2026-09-08. Target: **8/10 in each category**.

This assessment uses only direct inspection of the canyon concept and the three supplied clay renders. No implementation, earlier variants, reviews, or receipts were inspected. Clay color and the absence of a finished material are not scored. Physics, topology, and runtime performance cannot be established from these images.

## Decision: rebuild the hero geometry

**The asset is a usable broad arch blockout, but it does not yet read as the concept's natural sandstone bridge.** Retain the approximate opening envelope as a layout reference. Rebuild the visible rock masses, strata, fractures, and underside before material work. The current primary impression is a shallow slab with an arch cut through it; surface shading does not overcome its regular outer boundaries and nearly continuous inner rim.

**Mean: 2.8/10. None of the five criteria meets the 8/10 target.** These scores describe the geometry's visible suitability for this particular concept, not an assessment of the whole environment.

## What the concept requires

The concept's opening is bordered by overlapping angular strata with a varied thickness and direction. The left shoulder spreads into a large wall; the right springing descends through stepped, eroded rock. The span has substantial irregular mass above the opening, with fracture faces and ledges at several scales. Its underside is a broken rock surface, not a consistent band following the opening. Rock continues beyond both ends, so the arch reads as surviving geology within a canyon.

The supplied asset captures the broad empty space but only a small part of that surrounding form language. A detached asset cannot prove its final canyon integration, but its currently abrupt support boundaries provide little shaped transition to work with.

## Geometry corrections, in order

1. **Recompose the large masses.** Build a thicker, asymmetric crown and broad shoulders with varying front-to-back depth. Give the left and right abutments different widths, slopes, and broken profiles. Avoid a constant-depth slab. Shape shoulders that can overlap or disappear into canyon walls.
2. **Make strata change the outline.** Establish several dominant beds of unequal thickness and modestly varied inclination. Project selected hard layers; recess adjacent softer layers. Continue these beds around side faces and across the underside so they read as volumes. Do not make every band equally prominent or evenly spaced.
3. **Construct fractures as broken planes.** Use a small number of convincing major fissures with angular exposed faces, varied widths, and partial terminations. Let them interrupt and offset layers locally. Replace the evenly serrated left crack edge with a few deliberate chips and larger irregular breaks. Avoid a regular grid of similarly sized rounded blocks.
4. **Rebuild the soffit and opening edge.** Replace the continuous rim with overlapping rock shelves, localized undercuts, missing wedges, and unequal overhangs. Vary the opening contour at large and medium scales without filling the clear passage with indiscriminate noise. The front and rear opening edges should have different visible profiles.
5. **Add medium-scale erosion after the masses work.** Break exposed corners, step back selected beds, and add sparse angular spalls. Blend support bases into irregular skirts or wall transition volumes. Fine grain can remain a material task; ledges, deep recesses, silhouette chips, and the coarse opening contour require geometry.

## Limits a material cannot fix

Material work cannot supply the missing shoulder volume, front-to-back variation, broken opening outline, projecting strata, or irregular feet. It can clarify color, grain, and fine cracks, but the present silhouette and uninterrupted underside would remain visible from the quarter and underside angles. Do not attempt to meet the target by increasing contrast in the existing bands.

A subsequent clay review should show the new silhouette, continuous stratified volumes, and angular fracture depth from these same three angles. That would be the evidence needed to reconsider hero-asset readiness. The unseen rear face and final environment integration remain unassessed.
