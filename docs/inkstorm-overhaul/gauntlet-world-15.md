## Five highest-impact visible fixes

1. **Rebuild the scenic reveal of each major route event.** At salt run, expose a long winding path with receding fin formations instead of stopping the view at the first crest. At fork, stage two unmistakable visible paths around one substantial eroded divider, including the elevated right continuation. At launch, reveal a basin below the vehicle with overlapping cliff edges, intermediate formations, and a large settlement distributed across several distant shelves. At finish, show banking, a readable drop, and the next gantry. Use foreground overlap, middle-distance landmarks, and smaller distant features to establish scale; filling sky with a single large prop will not produce the same result.

2. **Replace the repeated shelf-rock language and empty ground transitions with authored geology.** The long regular horizontal slabs recur in grid, salt, canyon, fork, launch, finish, and live-drive. Break them into varied mass families: narrow fins, broad fractured mesas, tapered stacks, undercuts, and irregular arches. Add secondary breaks, talus fans, chipped edges, gravel, and partly buried rocks where cliffs meet sand. Give the road several visible scales of wear—broad wheel paths, ruts, coarse patches, and compacted edges—so it no longer reads as one finely striped carpet surrounded by smooth orange banks.

3. **Give built structures enough material and assembly detail for their screen size.** The grid gantry's huge flat beams and foundry's smooth cylinders need articulated joints, layered flanges, fasteners, supports, service access, cable runs, uneven panel edges, and controlled rust/dust accumulation. Dress the grid hangar openings with recognizable machinery and overlapping foreground objects; extend the foundry with offset bridges and pipe crossings through several depths. Replace the finish's sparse miniature rail segments with a continuous, materially substantial barrier integrated with the cliff. Detail must describe function, wear, and scale rather than scatter indiscriminate small shapes.

4. **Light the world as connected spaces with a deliberate value hierarchy.** Establish warmer direct light on selected rock and metal faces, cooler occluded recesses, contact darkening at structural joins, and stronger but coherent sun/shadow divisions across the road. In the canyon, make the far opening and road ribbon the clear luminous destination. In the foundry, add restrained visible pools from fixtures and machinery while preserving dark depth behind pipework. Use atmospheric separation through several distances in the launch basin. Avoid making every distant form the same pale purple or every near metal face a uniformly flat red/blue region.

5. **Finish the shared vehicle and remove visibly unintegrated foreground effects.** The engine silhouette is legible, but broad clean bands, sparse surface articulation, simplified inlet fans, and uniformly pale bodywork look much less substantial than the target's nested machinery. Add meaningful layered rings, deep intake recesses, exposed equipment, seams, heat/wear variation, and a more complete cockpit/pilot treatment. The live-drive image exposes two flat black rectangular shapes under the cockpit and large separate oval shadow patches; integrate the underside silhouette and ground shadows so they do not read as detached slabs and stamps. The bright energy connection currently resembles several uniform white zigzag ribbons; give it a controlled core, irregular branching, and restrained light response. Integrate or visually subordinate the green bars, white ground rings, and large hazard cards, especially where they obscure scenic focal points.
