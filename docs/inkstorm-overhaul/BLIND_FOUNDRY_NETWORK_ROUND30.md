Image-only review of the supplied reference and two candidates. No code, prior reviews, or implementation history was inspected. These findings concern visible still-image composition only; they make no claims about performance, motion, or geometry correctness.

- Reference: `docs/inkstorm-overhaul/concepts/06-foundry.png`

- Candidate B: `output/gauntlet/round-30-foundry-candidate/06-foundry.png`

## Verdict

**A wins. Strict target parity: FAIL for both.**

A has the stronger industrial landmark and the more coherent view into the foundry. B makes the nearest supports much heavier, but their broad, almost uninterrupted dark faces dominate the image and hide useful surrounding construction. The added visual mass does not produce a richer industrial scene in this view.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Comparative scene quality | A | The trussed gantry, layered horizontal members, differentiated support pieces, and hanging lights form a legible focal structure. B replaces that center-of-interest with two large dark slabs and relatively sparse overhead lines. |

## Concrete issues

1. **B loses the best landmark detail in A.** A's repeated diagonal braces, suspended cyan lamps, rails, and stepped support construction are immediately readable. B's replacement foreground is mostly broad blue surfaces and a few crossing pipes or beams. Its greater bulk reads as simplification at this camera position.
2. **Neither candidate presents the reference's continuous industrial canyon.** The reference has large side pipes, interconnected towers, catwalks, and several cross-route bridges at different distances. A provides one dominant near gateway; B provides a near pair of oversized supports. Neither shows a similarly convincing succession of connected structures receding through the center.
3. **The visible construction vocabulary is repetitive.** Both candidates rely heavily on similar smooth cylindrical tanks, dark horizontal bands, a small number of pipe shapes, and thin walkways. The reference varies vessel proportions, pipe sizes, braces, flanges, railings, cables, hanging hardware, and small facade detail much more extensively.
4. **The middle distance ends visually too soon.** The sand rise and large rocky background occupy the center where the reference reveals a long industrial passage, smaller structures, and successive shaded openings. This limits both depth and route anticipation in the stills.
5. **Large surfaces need visible scale cues.** B's nearest posts are the clearest problem, but both candidates also have large tank faces and retaining walls with relatively little readable secondary construction. Weathering alone does not supply the reference's abundance of platforms, fittings, seams, and attachments.
6. **Light and surface treatment remain short of the reference.** Both candidates have an appealing warm sky and cyan accents, but their foundry masses read more uniformly than the reference's intricate warm highlights, cool shadows, chipped surfaces, and layered atmospheric depth.
7. **The shared vehicle framing also differs markedly from the target.** The candidates show a small central vehicle from farther above and behind. The reference fills the lower frame with widely spread, richly articulated engines and an immediate cockpit view. This reduces overall image parity even independently of the foundry changes.

## Recommendation

Keep **A** as the stronger visible composition. Preserve its detailed gantry and open central aperture. The next visible improvement should be additional industrial connections and varied construction farther into the scene, with enough open center to reveal a continuing route. Enlarging foreground support faces as in B is a regression in this supplied view.
