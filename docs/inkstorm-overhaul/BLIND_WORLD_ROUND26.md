## Shared foreground finding

The racer is identifiable and separate from the ground in all seven captures. Its broad blue and cream panels, cables, vents, and engine pair can be read. It does not yet carry the image as a premium foreground subject: the blue housings have long quiet surfaces, the cyan exhaust ends flatten to almost solid disks, and the central body is largely a narrow cream shell. The current camera makes it feel suspended in the lower middle of the view, with a substantial apron of ground around it. The target uses a much stronger foreground silhouette and richer bright-to-dark separation.

Three shared corrections:

1. Give the existing licensed design stronger surface hierarchy: readable panel edges, recessed mechanical shadows, directional wear concentrated around joints, and restrained highlights that distinguish painted shell, exposed metal, and flexible cables.
2. Retain internal shape in the luminous engine ends. Establish a bright core, a darker mechanical rim, and controlled surrounding cyan light instead of nearly uniform pale disks. Preserve readability in both open sun and the canyon's purple shade.
3. Tune the chase framing and lighting together so the racer anchors the foreground more decisively while keeping the next turn visible. Preserve the full engine-pair silhouette through steering; the fork view currently overlaps the left engine and central body heavily.

## 01 Grid

The overhead gantry provides an immediate start-line frame and the forward lane is clear. The space around it reads as a sparse arrangement of simple sheds, walls, and poles. Large unmodulated metal faces and a mostly empty road remove the busy, inhabited pit-lane character of the target. The start lamps are dark balls with weak internal definition, and the gantry occupies much of the image without comparable material detail.

Priority corrections:

1. Build two or three dense, distinct pit clusters within the visible left and right middle ground: service equipment, stacked parts, varied awnings, hoses, and human-scale activity silhouettes. Keep the central racing corridor open; place detail where it reinforces depth at the edges.
2. Break up the gantry and hangar surfaces with believable construction: panel seams, bolted junctions, grime around braces, chipped edges, shaded interiors, and illuminated start-light lenses. The blue and rust paint needs several purposeful value bands instead of large flat fills.
3. Compose a sequence of near gantry, shaded pit fronts, smaller distant structures, and atmospheric canyon layers. Add directional surface marks and cast shadows that lead down the lane; the present broad tan ground does little to connect foreground to destination.

## 02 Salt run

The straight is legible between the markers, but it feels short and sparsely dressed. A low checkpoint frame and nearby crest terminate the view. The sky fills roughly half the frame, while the road lacks the directional texture and long receding rhythm that make the concept feel fast. Isolated repeated small rock stacks look placed along the track rather than part of an eroded landscape.

Priority corrections:

1. Extend the visible road into a long, gently bending valley with at least three overlapping scales of spire or mesa silhouette. Keep a distant route continuation visible beyond the nearest checkpoint and crest.
2. Replace regularly spaced small stacks with varied erosion groups: ledges, sloping debris fans, broken shards, and occasional large leaning forms. Use asymmetrical clusters and clear empty intervals instead of the same roadside token repeatedly.
3. Add broad painterly tonal variation and travel-aligned grooves to the road, plus stronger warm rim light and cool shadow grouping on the cliffs. The speed strokes at the image edges do not substitute for a ground plane that itself reads direction and scale.

## 03 Canyon and arch integration

The arch is visibly attached to the canyon walls and creates a recognizable passage in the main and approach captures. The supplementary sequence shows a continued corridor beyond it; there is no obvious visual disconnection of the arch itself. However, the framing is dominated by nearly uniform purple rock. Large similar wall masses overlap with weak tonal separation, and the route's rolling crest conceals much of the next bend. The target's dark arch framing a brighter, winding landscape is not achieved.

Priority corrections:

1. Stage the arch approach around a visible bright road exit: retain a darker foreground arch, illuminate a mid-distance curve, and expose smaller distant spires through the aperture. Keep the road line visible enough that the opening promises a destination rather than mostly sky.
2. Give the arch and adjacent cliffs distinct geological structure: ledge bands, undercuts, fracture planes, and broken talus at their feet. Replace broad same-purple wall fields with deliberate cool shadow masses and selective warm faces; avoid evenly noisy surface detail everywhere.
3. Integrate road edges into the canyon with irregular debris and worn shoulders, then reduce the dominance of the tall repeated marker cones. In the approach/under/beyond captures, those cones supply more readable depth punctuation than the geology or road itself.

## 04 Fork and entry integration

Two possible trajectories can be inferred: an elevated ramp to the left and the lower signed road. The junction does not yet read as a convincing split embedded in the canyon. The raised route resembles a narrow freestanding strip, and its continuation is hidden at the top of the rise. The fork-approach and fork-after-entry views expose long thin curb wedges and small angular fragments across the apparent joining area. This is a visible assembly problem, regardless of whether the driving surface behaves correctly.

Priority corrections:

1. Resolve the fork nose into a clean, continuous Y-junction. Remove the razor-thin diagonal curb ends and detached-looking wedges visible around the entry; make the road surfaces and shoulders meet with a deliberate, readable boundary.
2. Support and embed the elevated branch in a rock shelf or a fully authored bridge structure, and reveal a sustained section of both branches beyond the decision point. Each choice needs a clear destination and consistent edge guidance before the split.
3. Build the scene around one strong central geological divider, with a sheltered upper path and a more open lower path. Add stone transitions, structural wear, and shadow depth to the current smooth ramp and plain barriers; the giant repeated chevron boards should reinforce the scene rather than dominate it.

## 05 Launch, crest, and descent

The crest and descent images establish a hill with a road dropping away. They do not deliver the target's expansive reveal of a layered basin and substantial industrial destination. Two large mesas dominate the view, the foundry is a small distant cluster, and the route beyond the crest is only partly exposed. The descent shows a clearer leftward continuation but still a largely empty basin with little scale progression.

Priority corrections:

1. Recompose the crest reveal to expose the falling road and a substantial portion of the basin before the racer reaches the lip. A near ledge, descending mid-ground route, and low distant floor should make the height change obvious in one frame.
2. Enlarge and distribute the industrial destination into a coherent skyline: a principal dome or tower group, secondary tanks, and connective infrastructure across several terraces. Use atmosphere to separate it from the foreground instead of leaving a tiny cluster on one large mesa.
3. Add varied basin landforms and drifting dust pockets at multiple distances, with sunlit edges and cool occluded recesses. Break the large smooth mesa faces and empty tan floor into authored shapes without obstructing the landing corridor.

## 06 Foundry

The industrial theme is unmistakable, but the environment reads as repeated red cylinders and pipes with simple blue trim. Open pipe ends, flat platform edges, and large unshaded tank surfaces make it feel assembled from a small modular kit. The repeated start-style gantry is more prominent than any distinct foundry structure. The route rises into a mound before a rock wall, which limits the sense of a deep industrial corridor.

Priority corrections:

1. Replace the dominant start-style gate with a foundry-specific overhead composition: connected pipe runs, maintenance bridges, hanging machinery, and a few large secondary silhouettes. Give the visible pipe ends a purposeful destination or a designed broken/vented treatment.
2. Author the industrial materials and contact points: dense seam and flange shadows, worn metal edges, soot around outlets, localized warm work lights, and controlled cyan indicators. Reduce the broad flat red/blue bands and make tanks, pipes, support steel, and rock respond differently to light.
3. Open a readable route corridor through several overlapping layers of machinery. Lower or reshape the visual obstruction at the central mound, vary tank heights and spacing, and place debris/service details around foundations so structures sit convincingly in the terrain.

## 07 Finish

The left turn is clearly signed and the barrier follows it. The image primarily reads as another exposed hairpin. A small blocky tower provides some identity, but there is no strong finish destination or layered track infrastructure comparable to the target's weathered bank, fence, and distant overhead structure. The repeated chevron boards, smooth barrier bands, and isolated rock stacks dominate the roadside.

Priority corrections:

1. Establish a distinct finish destination visible through the turn: a substantial timing/finish structure, lighting, and a coherent spectator or service silhouette. It needs a clear focal hierarchy separate from ordinary track checkpoints.
2. Rework the visible barrier into an authored banked edge with construction joints, repairs, abrasion, dust accumulation, and varied rail supports. Tie it into the cliff and road shoulder instead of maintaining long clean color strips.
3. Improve the turn's composition with a close shaded wall or infrastructure layer, a sunlit road arc, and a lighter distant finish reveal. Vary adjacent rock shapes and cluster roadside details so the repeated signs and rock stacks no longer carry most of the scene's visual rhythm.

## Acceptance consequence

The canyon arch is visually integrated enough to judge as part of the world, and the road guidance is generally understandable. Those observations do not raise any section to the required art-quality threshold. The fork needs a direct geometry/assembly cleanup, while all seven sections need richer material hierarchy, stronger depth staging, and more specific environmental authorship. Reassess against the same original targets after those changes; do not substitute the existence of the named landmarks for their visual acceptance.
