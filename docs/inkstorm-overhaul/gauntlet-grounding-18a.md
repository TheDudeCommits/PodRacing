## Rock and structure grounding — 5.5/10

The actual images establish large rock masses and some useful directional shadows. The near track poles and suspended vehicle also cast readable shadows, so the scene is not uniformly detached from the ground. The missing quality is a convincing transition between the major forms and their supporting terrain.

1. **Rock bases terminate too abruptly.** In `02-salt-run.png`, the large left cliff meets an uninterrupted sand surface along a conspicuously clean lower boundary. The right needle and its pale backing cluster also disappear into the dune with little broken material around their feet. `live-drive.png` makes that isolated needle especially obvious. Compared with the target's layered shelves, debris slopes, and irregular sand-filled crevices, these contacts read as separate props inserted into a sand sheet. I cannot establish literal floating from these stills; the defect is the visible contact treatment.
2. **Adjacent rock forms do not read as one geology.** In `05-launch.png`, pale, crumpled boulder clusters sit beside dark, thin, blade-like pillars. Their color, surface scale, and shapes differ strongly without intermediate forms. In the target, pillars, cliffs, and rubble share a warm stratified rock language. The mismatch weakens the apparent relationship between supporting rocks and the settlement above.
3. **The settlement feels perched on a collection of pieces.** In `05-launch.png`, the large right building has an exceptionally straight, exposed underside and tall narrow supports. The smaller left platforms have similarly thin legs. Their feet and lower decks lack visually persuasive footings, retaining walls, rock-cut ledges, and dense attachment shadows. Visible supports exist, but the building-to-cliff connection is less convincing than the target's architecture embedded in terraces.
4. **Repeated small rock piles have weak local integration.** The brown piles along the salt run have compact, similar silhouettes and clean outlines against sand. Their bases need a little surrounding grit, embedded fragments, and stronger local contact rather than simply more copies.

**Next corrections, in order:**

- Give the most visible cliffs and needles broad, irregular buried bases with a graded spread of small rubble, partial sand cover, and crevice/contact shading. Break up the long clean intersection lines first.
- Unify the major rock families through shared warm strata, intermediate buttresses, and consistent erosion scale. Preserve silhouette variety while reducing the pale-cluster/dark-blade disconnect.
- Build a readable load path beneath the prominent settlement: terrace or retaining mass, broad footings, braces, and shaded attachment joints that visibly meet rock. Reduce the exposed ruler-straight underside where it makes the structure feel placed on top.

## Vehicle finish — 6.5/10

The twin-engine silhouette, blue/orange palette, dark ribbed cables, cyan intakes, and contrasting panel boundaries are clear across all three driving images and the garage. This is a recognizable authored vehicle. The remaining gap to the target is surface depth and convincing manufactured construction.

1. **Engines remain long, regular barrels.** Across the driving images, broad blue shells and uninterrupted orange rear bands dominate. The target has more convincing overlapping armor, exposed machinery, stepped housings, and broken silhouettes. The actual fins and small strips help, but do not yet overcome the smooth cylindrical foundation.
2. **Intakes read as flat graphic discs.** The large cyan circles, black annuli, and broad gray interior shapes have limited visible axial depth. The target's turbine assembly has several nested rims, dense vanes, dark cavities, and a concentrated luminous center. The actual intake needs that depth hierarchy more than additional overall glow.
3. **Material and wear variation are too shallow.** Large blue cockpit and engine panels mostly share a smooth, even finish. A few broad gray wear marks and orange chips are visible, but they do not convincingly distinguish painted armor, worn metal edges, hot machinery, and flexible cable. Broad black gaps also substitute for some of the recessed mechanical construction.
4. **The cockpit and pilot are noticeably simpler than the engine detail.** In the driving views, the broad nose is mostly uninterrupted, the pilot has very simple helmet/shoulder forms, and a conspicuous blue rectangular seat/backing panel appears behind the shoulders. The target has a deeply nested cockpit with upholstery, harness/body articulation, and layered surrounding armor.

**Next corrections, in order:**

- Rebuild the visible intake depth: recessed turbine, multiple offset rim layers, narrower vanes, fasteners, and a restrained bright core. Ensure it still reads at the garage's smaller display size.
- Break the long engine shells with substantial overlapping armor edges, believable vents, mounts, exposed mechanisms, and thickness at fins and panel ends. Concentrate detail near the front housing and cable attachment points where it is most visible.
- Establish distinct paint/metal/rubber responses and place wear at edges, seams, fasteners, and heat-exposed zones. Add cockpit nesting and replace the exposed rectangular seat read with a shaped seat enclosure and clearer pilot construction.

## Acceptance implication

**Neither bounded category clears 8/10.** More detail alone will not resolve the grounding score: the next captures need visibly better transitions at rock bases and settlement supports. For the vehicle, the clearest route upward is genuine intake/cockpit depth and material separation, rather than uniformly increasing small surface marks.
