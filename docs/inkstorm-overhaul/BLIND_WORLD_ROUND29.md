### 01 — Grid

**Strengths:** The broad metal gantry reads immediately as a start landmark. Riveted plates, bracing, hanging lights, workshop arches, poles, and rock towers make this recognizably a desert racing settlement. Warm sky against cool metal is coherent. The central dirt lane is easy to identify.

**Parity blockers:** The gantry dominates a comparatively empty street. The concept derives its scale from people, equipment, overlapping awnings, occupied workshop mouths, and several layers of settlement; those cues are weak or absent in the actual view. Repeated shelters are set well back behind broad sand banks, leaving very little close material storytelling. Much of the road has one gently varied surface value, rather than ruts, compacted lanes, broken margins, and localized shadows. The distant street does not accumulate the concept's depth. Large smooth support faces and simplified shed profiles remain conspicuous beside the detailed rock texture.

**Pilot/vehicle:** The paired engines and connecting cables are unmistakable, and the cyan hubs create useful contrast. The narrow, high chase presentation does not establish a readable seated pilot. Broad engine shells, small fittings, and restrained silhouette variation do not approach the concept's dense mechanical foreground.

**Next action:** Dress and reshape the near and middle pit street as one composition: shelter fronts close to the track, varied overlapping canopies, grounded service clusters, and a few clear human-scale silhouettes leading toward a populated distant center.

### 02 — Salt run

**Strengths:** The warm open landscape, tall rock silhouettes, distant industrial landmark, and repeated route markers communicate an exposed straight. Ground color distinguishes the driving corridor from the pale shoulders.

**Parity blockers:** The target is an expansive route with many receding, angled rock blades and a long visible trajectory. Here, a few large isolated masses flank an almost empty sand field, and the road disappears behind a low rise. The sky occupies a great deal of the composition without an equivalent layered skyline beneath it. Rock forms jump between very noisy crags and relatively smooth rounded walls; the near-left rock mass also presents a conspicuously blunt base edge. Identically styled small rock piles and tall tapered markers become the primary roadside rhythm. The thin rectangular gate contributes little place-specific identity.

**Pilot/vehicle:** The same narrow engine grouping and concealed pilot limit the foreground scale anchor. The small energy connection does not produce the concept's broad, visually connected machine silhouette.

**Next action:** Sculpt the route and surrounding rock distribution together so that several bends or undulations remain visible into the distance, supported by varied foreground fins, middle-distance spires, and pale distant silhouettes.

### 03 — Canyon

**Strengths:** This is the clearest landmark translation. A substantial rock arch bridges enclosing walls, and its opening frames a brighter sky. The surrounding vertical masses give the craft a credible small scale. The supplemental approach shows an open route beneath the arch, with additional rock layers visible through it. The under and beyond frames retain an identifiable corridor.

**Parity blockers:** The concept uses a broad, naturally layered arch, rich warm/cool striation, rubble shelves, and a luminous winding route visible beyond the opening. The actual arch and walls mostly form a uniform purple rock mass with fine surface noise. The road crest hides much of the downstream route and reduces the portal's reveal. The arch crown has lumpy, abrupt contours rather than a convincing hierarchy of large strata and smaller breaks. Close route pylons become extremely prominent against the vehicle. Broad, hard-edged polygonal shadow patches on the sand draw attention away from the rock forms. The beyond view is dominated by a large near-left wall and another generic rectangular gate rather than a composed vista.

**Pilot/vehicle:** The darker scene preserves the cyan hubs, but the central cockpit and pilot merge into a slim dark mechanical shape. No clear helmet, shoulders, or seated body is established from the race camera.

**Next action:** Make the opening frame a legible downstream S-curve with warm light on selected rock ledges and a darker foreground arch, then break the uniform walls into readable major strata, shelves, and grounded debris.

### 04 — Fork

**Strengths:** An elevated route on the left and a lower route to the right are visually distinguishable. Cyan posts associate with the raised branch, while chevrons describe the lower bend. The distant rocks provide a substantial backdrop.

**Parity blockers:** The defining concept composition—a monumental central rock splitting two natural paths—is missing. The actual raised branch reads as a separate, thin road ribbon placed over sand. The approach exposes its dark underside and simple supports. Its entry and side curbs terminate abruptly; a small detached-looking curb fragment is visible to the right in `fork-approach.png`. The long diagonal road edge remains conspicuous in `fork-after-entry.png`. Neither ground blending nor structural detail makes these joins feel finished. Repeated chevrons and angular barrier height changes dominate the lower route. Similar large crag silhouettes recur in the background. The branches are detectable, but their landscape relationship and visual invitation are weaker than in the target.

**Pilot/vehicle:** The angled craft exposes more side geometry, but its cockpit reads as a narrow wheel-like mechanical assembly. A seated pilot still is not readily legible. This is a visual-presence judgment, not a claim that no pilot geometry exists.

**Next action:** Reconstruct the visible split around one strong rock mass, give both branches deliberate terrain or engineered support, and resolve the junction's exposed slab edges and isolated curb pieces before adding more props.

### 05 — Launch

**Strengths:** A foundry silhouette occupies an elevated mesa and gives the scene a destination. Tall canyon masses and the warm sky convey a large landscape. `launch-descent.png` reveals a curving route down and left, so there is more visible route shape than the main frame alone supplies.

**Parity blockers:** None of the four launch stills delivers the concept's elevated view into a deep, densely layered basin. Approach and crest are dominated by a broad sand foreground and a relatively level horizon. The main view presents a few large walls and a modest industrial cluster rather than a valley populated by staggered mesas, bridges, shelves, dust layers, and a substantial foundry. The descent reveals a comparatively empty basin enclosed by smooth continuous walls. A long thin dark boundary across the right-hand basin is visually conspicuous; its cause cannot be established from the still. Identical roadside piles, narrow markers, and another rectangular gate do much of the scene organization. The cyan machine accents are coherent, but the vehicle lacks the target's pilot-led cockpit framing.

**Next action:** Lower the revealed basin, stage several rock and industrial layers at different distances and heights, and make the descending route visible from the crest. Judge the result again from all four supplied camera positions, not just one scenic angle.

### 06 — Foundry

**Strengths:** Large vessels, elbows, pipes, valves, ladders, railings, and elevated platforms make the industrial setting recognizable. Orange equipment, blue bands, cyan instrument details, and shadowed sand hold together as a palette. The craft remains separated from its immediate surroundings.

**Parity blockers:** The target is a deep, richly connected industrial canyon. The actual view reads as rows of similar simple tanks around a reused start-style gantry, with a large sand mound and rock wall closing the sightline. Multiple pipes face the road with featureless dark circular ends, emphasizing isolated components rather than an interconnected facility. Tank surfaces have some wear, but large areas remain flat and the silhouettes lack the concept's varied collars, service decks, secondary structures, and density. The prominent gantry lights repeat the grid's visual identity instead of establishing a foundry-specific overhead system. Simple dark retaining faces and coarse sand forms weaken the ground contacts. The road continuation beyond the mound is poorly expressed in this frame.

**Pilot/vehicle:** As elsewhere, broad blue shells and cyan outlets read clearly but the pilot remains visually unresolved. The sparse mechanical foreground does not balance the scale of the tanks as effectively as the concept machine.

**Next action:** Open a readable corridor through the facility, connect the major vessels with visible pipe networks, and layer two or three distinct overhead industrial structures down the sightline with localized warm and cyan light accents.

### 07 — Finish

**Strengths:** The curving edge barrier and left chevrons make the immediate turn direction clear. Tall rocks establish a desert cliff setting, and the dark observation structure adds a man-made landmark. The overall colors remain consistent with the rest of the course.

**Parity blockers:** The concept's banked road, weathered integrated safety wall, exposed outer drop, and framed gantry destination are not matched. The actual guardrail follows pronounced angular up-and-down steps, producing a coarse zigzag profile. Its smooth dark-red base and repeated large signs feel much simpler than the target's layered barrier construction. Broad sand undulations and sparse rock piles replace a strongly authored racing line and shoulder. Large similar background crags compress the view, and the small blocky tower does not provide an equivalent finish composition. The actual frame communicates a marked corner more readily than a distinctive concluding landmark.

**Pilot/vehicle:** Cyan hubs keep the machine visible, but its narrow silhouette and unreadable seated occupant repeat the other sections' foreground weakness.

**Next action:** Shape the corner, bank, outer drop, and barrier as one continuous structure; smooth accidental-looking barrier height changes and place a distinct destination gantry or equivalent landmark where the curve naturally leads the eye.

## Scoped conclusion

There is a coherent desert-industrial world here, with readable category-level landmarks and a consistent warm/cool palette. Those strengths do not establish strict concept parity. The supplied actuals remain substantially behind in authored depth, integrated route construction, material hierarchy, believable industrial and settlement density, and pilot-led vehicle presence. Repeated rocks, markers, gates, and smooth sand carry too much of the visual identity. The fork's exposed joins and the finish barrier's coarse profile are concrete visible finish defects; the launch lacks its defining vista.

The highest-value world work is to resolve the fork junction, compose the launch reveal, and establish genuine depth in the grid and foundry before increasing incidental detail. Across the course, replace repeated visual rhythms with section-specific terrain and structures, then make the pilot and cockpit readable at the actual race-camera scale. **Visual acceptance remains FAIL for these stills.** No conclusion is made about the running game's behavior or performance.
