# Blind visual review — world gauntlet, round 2

**Verdict: FAIL for the supplied target fidelity.** The current stills establish a recognizable stylized desert racer, a clear twin-engine craft silhouette, and some useful route cues. They do not yet deliver the targets' authored terrain, convincing material differentiation, cinematic light, or dense industrial world. The canyon is substantially closer in spatial character than the foundry. This verdict is about visible target fidelity, not whether the game is functional or enjoyable.

Reviewed directly from the seven supplied images only. No code or previous reports were read, and no browser was used. The two concept images are visual targets, not evidence that their exact density can be rendered within any particular performance budget.

## Five hardest defects, in priority order

1. **The environments lack the targets' defining structures and depth hierarchy.** The canyon's near walls successfully enclose the route, but large faceted wall slabs replace the target's arch, layered ledges, rubble shelves, and winding, progressively distant vista. The foundry is the most severe mismatch: one large rectangular gate, repeated pale uprights, and open desert dominate the frame. There is no comparably readable network of tanks, pipes, catwalks, overhead machinery, or nested industrial passages. **Fix:** author one unmistakable canyon hero passage with a rock arch, asymmetric ledges, and a visible route beyond it. Build a foundry composition around large cylindrical vessels and bent overhead pipes, then connect them with supported catwalks and smaller service elements. Stage foreground, middle-distance, and distant silhouettes deliberately; increase density around the route and keep its driving corridor clear. The key is distinct structures with a purpose, rather than more copies of the current pillars.

2. **Large surfaces read as stretched procedural color bands and exposed triangulation.** Rock towers repeat the same pointed silhouette and horizontal striping; prominent triangular wedges break the cliffs without convincing erosion logic. The pale road, dunes, and rock shoulders share similar broad color treatment. The foundry's pale columns have little visible metallic identity. These surfaces cannot support the targets' close views of stratified stone, gritty ground, and worn metal. **Fix:** use a small set of deliberately different rock forms, align strata across their faces, and break the ground into readable road wear, coarse shoulders, and rubble deposits. Give industrial assets a separate palette and response, with large panel seams, edge wear, dark joint recesses, and localized grime. Retain stylization, but make stone, sand, paint, and metal distinguishable at racing-camera distance. Reserve fine detail for surfaces near the route.

3. **Lighting flattens the world and the sky competes with it.** Most surfaces sit in a similar bright pastel range, with weak contact darkening and little distant atmospheric separation. Large cream-and-mauve sky shapes compete with the geology. In the canyon frame, a perfectly white celestial disc appears against the cliff face, reading as an overlay rather than a light source situated behind the world. The targets instead use warm directional light, substantial cool shadow areas, and luminous distance to organize the route. **Fix:** establish one consistent key-light direction, strengthen contact and cast shadows where cliffs meet the road, preserve darker material recesses, and progressively soften/simplify distant geometry with haze. Reduce sky contrast and scale its details to support the landscape. Verify celestial occlusion against cliff silhouettes. Keep cyan energy highlights locally bright without bleaching the surrounding craft and ground.

4. **The craft has good screen presence but insufficient mechanical and pilot credibility.** The two engines frame the lane clearly, and cyan/orange/navy separation works. However, their large opaque pale end caps resemble low-detail nozzles; the energy connection is two smooth luminous bands. The pilot reads as a round head and narrow torso perched above a bowl-like hull. Against the targets' recessed turbines, layered housings, cockpit, and flexible connections, the result feels assembled from simple components. **Fix:** add visible depth to each engine opening with a dark recess, concentric rings, blades or a bright contained core. Build a continuous cockpit rim and seat around the pilot, improve helmet/shoulder silhouettes, and add large purposeful housing seams and cable attachments. Replace uniform energy ribbons with a restrained irregular core and glow that still leaves the road visible. Preserve the current strong silhouette rather than obscuring it with tiny details.

5. **Hazard graphics announce danger more clearly than the world explains it.** In the final image, multiple rockfall badges float high in the scene while outlined faceted debris overlaps the craft and orange circles occupy the road underneath. It is difficult to distinguish the incoming threat, actual impact area, and aftermath from this still. The prominent “SANDSTORM ACTIVE” banner is not supported by an equally clear visible dust/visibility condition in the frame. Green route markers are clear, but heat/damage text in the lower-right panel is too dark to read reliably. **Fix:** attach each warning to a visible source and projected impact region; use a restrained ground telegraph before impact, solid shaded debris during it, then dust and fragments afterward. Keep the dangerous footprint readable around the craft. Give sandstorm conditions a coherent dust layer and reduced distant contrast while preserving nearby pylons. Raise status-label contrast and avoid stacking floating badges without clear spatial anchors.

## Target comparison and acceptance boundary

- **Canyon: FAIL.** It communicates a narrow canyon and a navigable bend. It does not yet reproduce the target's landmark quality, geological layering, light/shadow structure, or sense of discovering a larger world through an arch.
- **Foundry: FAIL, largest gap.** It communicates gates and columns in the same desert setting. The target communicates a working industrial district whose structures define the racing space. This requires different major assets and composition, not a final color grade.
- **Salt run/live start:** route perspective and near/far rock overlap give some scale. Repetitive towers, exposed empty shoulders, and an oversized high-contrast sky limit the sense of place. No salt-run concept was supplied, so there is no direct section-target verdict for it.
- **Hazard final:** an event is visibly happening, but its state and boundaries need a cleaner visual hierarchy. A single screenshot cannot establish warning lead time, collision fairness, or the temporal clarity of the full sequence.

**Release implication:** do not describe these stills as meeting the supplied canyon/foundry visual targets. A narrower claim of a stylized playable implementation may be appropriate if independently verified. Stills do not certify FPS, audio, animation quality, steering, driving feel, collision behavior, or actual runtime stability; none of those receives a pass here.
