## Fork evidence

**Ground contact and construction:** A's approach shows a long, thin ramp slab over large gaps and sparse rectangular supports. B fills that exposed edge with a continuous faceted rock mass that meets the surrounding sand. The same improvement is clear along the left edge of `04-fork`. However, the straight slab edging, planar purple retaining face, and abrupt orange bank against the central rock still read as separate assembled surfaces. In `fork-after-entry`, B's orange bank meets the crag with a sharp, flat-looking junction, unlike the target's rubble, worn ledges, and continuous rock-to-road transition.

**Lower-route advance visibility:** Both approach views strongly advertise the rising left ramp. The lower option reads mainly as an undifferentiated sand area to its right, ending visually in barriers and large chevrons. The image does not reveal a comparably clear lower ribbon continuing around the landmark before commitment. B does not materially improve that cue in the matched approach, hero, or after-entry images. Its extra `fork-downstream` image reveals a broader lower passage bending left around the rock, but a later unpaired view cannot establish that the choice was legible earlier or that A was worse there.

**Target structure:** The target places a monumental central rock immediately at a broad, readable split, with two continuous roads visibly diverging around it and an elevated route threading through an enclosing rock arch. Both candidates instead show an exposed manufactured ramp on the left, an open sandy expanse on the right, distant repeated crags, and a large sky area. B's foundation fixes a local construction weakness without resolving this composition gap.

## Foundry evidence

**Depth:** In both `06-foundry` images, strong near overhead beams and side tanks establish an industrial setting, but the central road terminates visually at a close sandy rise and cliff. The target allows the eye to follow a long central corridor through multiple progressively smaller bridges, pipes, tanks, and canyon walls. In `foundry-middle`, B adds lateral pipe continuity across successive tank groups. In `foundry-exit`, B adds substantial overhead pipe diagonals that reduce A's empty upper field. Those are useful depth cues, although they remain large strokes over a short, open sequence rather than the target's sustained corridor.

**Joins and construction:** B's middle view replaces several exposed dark circular ends with elbows and connecting spans, making the facility look more connected. The new exit pipe network still has abrupt angular turns and congested crossings: notably the dark blue diagonal terminating near the orange pipe above the central-right tank. Visible supports, attachment plates, and consistent flange treatment would make these junctions more legible as designed assemblies. Existing platforms and near columns generally show contact with terrain, but rectangular plinths, uniform sand embankments, and repeated tank footprints retain a placed-on-terrain appearance.

**Target density and material:** The target has varied tower proportions, maintenance structures, rich structural overlap, visibly weathered metal, rock shoulders, localized warm lamps, and cyan fixtures extending down the corridor. Both candidates rely on closely repeated cylindrical tanks and pale square stacks, with large simple surfaces and sparse detail. B's pipe additions help continuity but do not erase that material and silhouette gap.

## Vehicle composition

The vehicle appears materially unchanged between A and B. It occupies a narrow central band, with long simple engine bodies, small rear apertures, thin connecting cables, and a tall narrow rear body. In the fork images the body is also strongly skewed relative to the engine pair. The targets place a broad cockpit and visible pilot at the bottom center, with large, widely spread engines, prominent fins, layered mechanical detail, and substantial cyan exhaust faces filling much of the lower image width. Even allowing for different capture aspect ratios, the difference in camera distance, engine spacing, pilot visibility, and silhouette is large. Neither set reaches strict target vehicle parity.

## Top three concrete defects

1. **The fork does not show the lower route clearly enough before entry.** In both `fork-approach` and `04-fork`, the raised left ramp has an explicit surface and edge, while the lower continuation blends into the right-hand sand and disappears behind the crest, vehicle, and barriers. Expose a continuous lower road curve and its next segment from the approach camera, with readable edges around the separating landmark. Keep B's grounded ramp foundation while integrating its seams into the rock.
2. **The foundry lacks a visible long corridor, and the added pipe junctions still look abrupt.** The shared hero composition faces a short mound/cliff closure; B's middle and exit improve pipe continuity but retain chunky angular joins and repeated modules. Establish successive smaller structural spans along a visible road line, then give the blue/orange exit crossing and other conspicuous connections explicit elbows, flanges, and support logic.
3. **The vehicle framing and silhouette remain far from both targets.** The narrow, distant engine pair and tall rear body leave much of the lower frame as empty road, with no clearly visible pilot. Bring the chase composition closer to the target's broad lower-frame footprint and develop the engine fins, cockpit/pilot, machinery, and luminous rear faces as a coherent silhouette.

Still images cannot prove driving physics, collision safety, route traversability, ground-contact behavior over time, continuous motion quality, or FPS. The recommendations above address visible composition and construction only.
