The filename `live-drive.png` does not change that evidence limit: a still cannot establish continuous gameplay, responsiveness, stability, or 40–60 fps. Those remain unassessed.

## Standard

An 8 requires plausible finished-game presentation with composition, material language, and environmental detail comparable to the goals. Pixel-for-pixel reproduction and generated microdetail are unnecessary. A shared orange/cyan palette and the presence of the same object categories are insufficient. Scores below assess the current image against that standard, rather than scoring the generated goal as a shipped game.

## What already works

The warm sky, purple rock shadows, and cyan/coral pod establish a consistent broad palette. The engines have readable rings, panels, fasteners, fins, and wear variation. The canyon enclosure and foundry silhouettes create some location identity. HUD typography is stylistically compatible with the garage. These are useful foundations, but the vehicle and UI currently carry substantially more authored detail than the environment around them.

## Three highest-impact changes

1. **Rebuild the major views around depth and location-specific silhouettes.** Give Salt Run a long visible route with several staggered distance planes and varied leaning spires. Make Canyon's opening frame a bright winding exit. Show both Fork routes separated by a rock pier, with a visible elevation difference. Put Launch above a genuinely readable valley and embed the settlement across rocky terraces. Restore Finish's banked edge, open drop, and gate ahead. These changes should be judged first as broad light/dark shapes; adding small props to the current shallow views will not solve their composition.

2. **Replace uniform rock shelves and broad smooth surfaces with authored material structure.** Vary cliff profiles, stratum thickness, fracture direction, overhangs, and broken bases. Add deliberate painted value/color breaks and selective edge accents at medium scale, then finer cracks and chips where the camera approaches. Break the road's uniform longitudinal surface with compacted racing lines, exposed stone, dusty margins, and transitions into rubble. Use directional shade and contact darkening to anchor rocks, terrain, and structures; distant layers should soften and separate. Preserve the palette while bringing the world's surface treatment closer to the pod's richer panel language.

3. **Make the start area and foundry look assembled, used, and inhabited.** Cluster equipment, crates, spares, cables, awnings, and scale cues around hangar mouths; make a few interiors readable instead of uniformly black. At Foundry, connect pipes to believable destinations, add joints/brackets/supports, vary tank proportions, and layer catwalks and overhead crossings into the distance. Give metal broad weathering, seam shadows, exposed edge wear, and localized light accents. Integrate or conceal the large plain dark platforms and settlement plinth. Concentrate detail at structural junctions and road edges so the driving corridor remains readable.

Secondary cleanup after those three changes: refine the visible pilot/cockpit, soften the pod's plain dark underside, and resolve the flat yellow shape at Fork and pale geometric effects at Finish so their intended visual roles read clearly.

## Regressions and limits

**No temporal regressions can be established.** Earlier-round images were intentionally excluded. The yellow shape at Fork and the pale effects at Finish are visible present-image issues, not proven regressions. Scores describe only the supplied stills and cannot determine whether a mismatch is caused by world construction, camera placement, staging, lighting, or a transient effect.

The round does not meet the requested concept-comparable world presentation. Its broad style is recognizable, but its environmental composition, material hierarchy, and density still read as an intermediate game build.
