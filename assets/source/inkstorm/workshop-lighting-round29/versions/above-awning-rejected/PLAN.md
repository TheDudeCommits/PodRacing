## Candidate behavior

`runtime-lighting.patch` modifies only the existing surface material and passes the family name at the existing batch-material construction site. Every other caller retains the current default shader. Two compile-time family variants contain the local arithmetic; rocks, terrain, road, foundations, gantry, pipe banks, and vista machinery do not gain lights. The two families keep their current materials and instanced draws.

Only the owning bay evaluates a light, selected through disjoint X receiver intervals. A soft receiver envelope limits it to the actual interior and covered apron, and clips above the hood and behind the rear wall. Distance falloff has finite support, an 8 m soft core, and a smooth squared cutoff. The downward beam has a broad, softly cut edge. There is no time animation, additional bloom, global ambient uplift, texture sample, or light array.

The warm contribution is `wornBase * (1,.74,.43) * strength`, capped at 0.66. This retains each vertex-painted material and wear pattern instead of painting the interior orange. Pure front-facing diffuse rejects away-facing normals; there is no wrap term. Existing cyan service areas are excluded from the extra diffuse term. Only the small warm lens bottom receives a bounded self-emission floor `(0.54,0.31,0.105)` before the existing atmosphere/color-space steps. This is an artistic radiant-strength approximation, not a calibrated lumen or color-temperature simulation.

World distance uses the lengths of each full model/instance basis vector. Fixture positions follow the transformed geometry, while range stays in world metres. The local normal is divided by those scales before normalization. That agrees with an independent world-space normal-matrix calculation for all 12 sampled orthogonal transforms, including yaw and unequal frontage/height/depth scales; maximum dot-product discrepancy was below 2e-15. Sheared transforms are unsupported. Current placement composition uses orthogonal position/yaw/scale transforms.

## Costs and limitations

- Existing batches/material count and texture memory stay unchanged. The two material variants add two program-cache variants and one vec3 varying only to those variants. At a covered receiver, the fragment adds one line-light evaluation: a distance inverse square root, two normalizations, arithmetic and smooth cutoffs. Other family shaders compile the candidate out. There are no per-frame CPU allocations, new scene lights, shadow maps, draw calls, or rendering passes.
- Fixtures add 72 triangles per family, 504 triangles across the current three pit complexes and four districts if all are drawn. The construction candidate's district reaches 17,494 triangles, within its 17,500 cap. Actual exported counts belong to its geometry receipt.
- **The local lamps do not cast occlusion shadows.** Receiver bounds and surface-normal rejection prevent common exterior/roof leakage, but cannot stop a lit floor underneath an intervening engine, cabinet, or person. The existing sun-shadow result is untouched; multiplying task light by sun visibility would incorrectly extinguish working lights inside the hangar. Reject conspicuous light-through-geometry in the actual A/B; these math checks do not prove its absence.
- The pools act only on each family's own combined mesh. The separate foundation and terrain cannot receive this light. The interior slab can; if the pool is visibly cut by its edge, adjust the authored pool within that slab or reject it. Do not expand the family scope to light the ground everywhere.
- A task fixture over the existing rear hoist mostly reveals original engine tops and curved enamel/steel. Engine apertures face away and remain dark. The new front-apron engine is farther from it and receives a much weaker contribution. Do not claim that brighter hardware solves equipment articulation or terrain burial.
