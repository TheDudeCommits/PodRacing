# Connected launch ridges — local implementation awaiting visual judgment

7 September 2026. Round 18's blind critic scored launch composition 5.6/10 and finish 4.3/10. Its actual screenshot showed separated pale boulders, pointed accents, a shallow-looking sand basin and a refinery perched on long exposed supports. The target instead uses connected escarpments, a descending route, several depths of terrain and industry seated in a cliff settlement.

## Landscape change

`LaunchBasinPlan.ts` now authors three continuous spines in the actual launch tangent's coordinates: a five-station near west rim, five-station western basin shelf and six-station eastern refinery ridge. Both the physical field and scanned face placements consume those stations. Width and top elevation vary along each spine; two restrained erosion terms break their edge without producing another set of isolated polygon islands.

`extendLaunchBasin` retains the existing central 160 m basin cut and restores surviving ridge material only outside every protected race corridor. The earlier min-only composition could never recover a shallow shelf where an earlier cut had already lowered the field further. The new blend can restore an off-road cut toward an authored shelf height, while remaining at or below the original terrain surface. The field still has exactly two R32F grids (417² and 257² at 6 m spacing), 959,752 bytes. No third texture, new per-frame sampler or render-only height surface exists.

Main/branch lane texels plus ten metres, one full 6 m cell diagonal and four metres of normal/filter margin keep their previous values. The exact previous interpolated launch-grid values were retained over **532,296 samples**: 8,192 dense main stations across seven lane/shoulder positions plus every branch segment, each with 0.85 m physics and 1.15 m MRT normal probes. SHA-256 over the ordered little-endian Float64 samples is `8b719ceac7e26597a4c33989e6a1a036d2dcfb703812313436838b150d221b57` before and after; maximum difference is zero. `launchRidgeRound18.ts` preserves this historical baseline independently of the new implementation. Finish/fork texture changes belong to the parallel fork task and are not covered by this launch-only claim.
