This view also makes B's remaining finish limitations unmistakable: broad blank interiors, thin unarticulated frames and poles, minimal engine hardware, small rigid figures, and repeated props dominate even at close range.

## Concrete next defects, in priority order

1. **Resolve the left bay's terrain intrusion in view 1.** Show one continuous usable floor under the complete engine platform, service cabinets, and support feet. No engine face, cabinet, or figure should be partially swallowed by a mound. Recheck all three supplied locations; the cleaner third view does not demonstrate the first is fixed.
2. **Make the floor and shelter visibly belong to the terrain.** Give the apron an intentional threshold or ramp and a believable perimeter transition into sand. Show base plates or footings at arch and pole contacts, and readable joints where frames connect. The current combination of a broad raised slab and thin supports still feels assembled from independent simple shapes.
3. **Build one convincing maintenance station before adding more copies.** Give its engine a supported cradle, differentiated casing sections, a few substantial mechanical fittings, and visible service connections. Arrange a workbench, parts storage, a task light, and a figure around a legible repair action. Each added item should explain what happens in the bay.
4. **Articulate the shelter and awning construction.** Introduce meaningful panel seams, ribs, braces, and fastening points. Make cloth read through attachment, edge thickness or hems, tension, and local folds. The large blue canopy and dark inner shells currently expose how little construction detail exists.
5. **Separate the materials and local lighting.** Use a coherent scale of paint chips and exposed edges on equipment, dusty accumulation at floor contact, and a discernible difference between cloth and painted metal. Add warm workshop light that reveals the interior and equipment; more scattered surface mottling alone will not match the concept's finish.
6. **Replace repetition with purposeful variation.** Give the adjoining bay a different service stage, engine arrangement, storage pattern, and occupied work zone. Avoid identical pairs of cabinets and neatly repeated rows of loose blocks. Retain clear access paths while filling selected areas with functionally connected detail.
