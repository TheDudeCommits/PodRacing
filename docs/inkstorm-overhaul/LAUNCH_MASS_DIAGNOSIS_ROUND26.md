# Launch mass diagnosis — round 26

The two giant smooth masses are **the signed physical terrain**, specifically `west-basin-shelves` and `industrial-east-ridge` in `src/game/race/LaunchBasinPlan.ts:32`. The left mass becomes the enormous central mesa in `launch-descent.png`; the industrial mass moves off the right side. The smaller jagged foreground forms are scanned `canyon-buttress` instances. Their fractured silhouettes and smaller footprints distinguish them from the broad terrain faces. The `near-west-rim` contributes near framing, not the distant paired mesas.

I directly inspected the concept and `output/gauntlet/round26-launch-v1/{05-launch,launch-crest,launch-descent}.png`. The concept has staggered towers, projecting ledges and visible gaps between depths. The candidate's smooth front envelopes dominate even where smaller scans add relief. This is a geometry problem that material changes cannot resolve.

The smooth shape is explicitly authored in `launchRidgeSurface()` (`LaunchBasinPlan.ts:145–167`). Distance to each clamped spine segment produces rounded capsule ends. A single cap contributes 83% of the height blend, with 17% broad talus. Its quintic transition spans `.36 * width`: about **88–103 m** on the west range and **81–104 m** on the east range. Both ends of that slope ease smoothly into the plateau and foot. The sinusoidal buttress offset moves the same continuous cap edge; it does not introduce separate projecting slabs or open notches through the front. These are the terrain function's buttresses, not the scanned mesh asset.

Ravines exist, but their placement and operation explain the weak result. They subtract 32–96 m from the crown and pinch the cap edge by only `.10 * width` (roughly 23–29 m). They retain the continuous talus and much of the cap. At the first west station, the first ravine is 102 m farther along the spine; at the first east station it is 76 m farther. Both distances exceed the respective ravine halfwidth even allowing the 8 m bend term. The nearest leading noses therefore survive. The first two west ravine centers project to launch x26.4% and x28.4%, only about 0.7–2.6% of image width away from the front station's column, while lying 102 m and 244 m farther away. This supports **rear cuts occluded by the leading cap** as a material contributor. The images show partial crown saddles, so it would be wrong to say every ravine disappeared. An exact first-hit occlusion measurement was deliberately deferred during the full-race benchmark; the projection alone is not that proof.

Clipmap interpolation can soften edges, but it is not the primary explanation for these smooth 80–104 m fronts. The authored field is bilinear at 6 m. `TerrainSystem.setCourseGulfField()` (`src/render/terrain/TerrainSystem.ts:208`) caps visible-valley levels at **12 m**, with additional cell-center samples in levels 3 and 4, and uses 24 m only at the outer horizon. The nominal 78–124 m ravines span multiple current cells. The former 48 m interpolation problem has already been addressed. Raising mesh density alone would reproduce the present smooth cap more faithfully. The right settlement's three flat bench cores also legitimately retain broad tops; their interiors must stay intact.

**One surgical change:** replace the two distant ranges' leading, basin-facing cap envelopes with an explicitly authored **broken escarpment profile** inside `launchRidgeSurface()`. Keep the existing spines, summit heights, connected talus, near ridge, and bench composition. In the front zones (initial authoring ranges: west forward 650–1120/right -620..-200; east forward 330–900/right 210..640), use three unequal polygonal setback contours with different termination points. Their fronts should form broad projecting shelves separated by 40–80 m recesses, and two or three 60–100 m wide slots should open all the way through the leading cap toward the talus. Drop the exposed slots by roughly 60–100 m rather than merely lowering hidden crowns. Use short, near-linear 20–28 m cliff transitions between ledges, with a small easing join, instead of one 90 m quintic roll. Those dimensions are starting design bounds, not approved terrain values; inspect their actual projections before selecting them. The heightfield can produce steep stepped faces and deep plan recesses, but cannot create overhangs.

Concrete implementation scope: add bounded escarpment contour/slot data beside `RIDGE_RAVINES` and apply it to the two named ridge contributions before the existing bench blend in `LaunchBasinPlan.ts`. Leave `CourseGulfField.extendLaunchBasin()` and its lane/filter mask, curved 240 m basin floor, central reveal and domain taper unchanged (`CourseGulfField.ts:263–295`). Leave the padded bench cores and their final precedence unchanged. Do not add another wall of scan instances, alter the road/camera, or globally sharpen all terrain. Retain `InkstormVista` and `TerrainSystem` geometry budgets.

After the freeze, prove the change with the recorded camera poses: reconstruct the exact camera aim, ray-test actual displaced clipmap triangles in the launch left-face ROI (roughly x17–41%, y24–58%), right-face ROI (x58–100%, y20–60%), and descent central-face ROI (x30–69%, y7–50%). Record first-hit world/launch-local coordinates before and after and show that the changed hits belong to these two terrain ranges. An isolated terrain/scan ID capture can confirm ownership; save and restore visibility immediately. Compare first hits against the 6 m physical field to separate occlusion from interpolation error. Require changed silhouette/depth in those ROIs, not just changed ridge tops outside the view. Then repeat actual launch/crest/descent captures, preserve the existing 532,296-sample corridor/probe hash and full bench-flatness tests, verify the central bowl and finish grid unchanged, and rerun the normal driving/performance gates.
