## Focused acceptance checks

**Fork simultaneous route legibility: 6/10 — below the bar.** Both routes can be located at once in fork-approach and fork-after-entry: the left option rises on a slab with cyan markers, and the right follows the sand beside the chevron barrier. That is useful visible evidence of two alternatives. Their junction is not cleanly authored: narrow exposed rail/edge pieces extend into the entry, the left slab looks laid across the sand, and the right option does not have an equally distinct entrance. The target communicates two continuous routes around a massive central geological divider; this candidate more readily reads as a main bend with an optional ramp. I cannot infer whether either route is usable from these stills.

**Launch connected terrain: visually present, with a narrow still-image pass.** Approach, crest, and descent show a continuous-looking sandy road over a hill into a leftward descending sweep; I do not see a clear open gap in the road surface in these views. This establishes only visible continuity. The surrounding terrain remains broad, shallow, and sparsely articulated, so the launch's overall composition still fails.

**Launch grounded industry: 4.5/10 — fail.** The distant factory has a horizontal foundation-like strip and is associated with a rock mass, but no convincing apron, retaining structure, access route, supporting terrace, or larger connected industrial settlement is readable. Its base contacts are too weak to establish the target's sense of a factory built into canyon geology. I am not claiming the model literally floats; the visual grounding is insufficient.

Warm lighting links the palette, but does not resolve the different material vocabularies.

## Top four remaining fixes

1. **Build the launch vista as a connected canyon composition.** Replace the almost empty horizon with substantial near/mid/far terraces and spires, a clearly descending route visible beyond the crest, and an industrial district founded on readable rock shelves. Give the factory an apron, retaining/support structures, access paths, and connected secondary installations. The factory should feel like a destination in the landscape rather than a lone prop above it.
2. **Unify rock form and material scale across the course.** The crumpled pale clusters, smooth towers, and swollen canyon walls currently look like different asset families. Establish layered sandstone strata, large fractured planes, consistent erosion, and smaller debris concentrated at believable bases. Keep distant masses simpler without making them unrelated silhouettes. The current repetitive lumpy rocks are conspicuous in salt run, fork, launch, and finish.
3. **Rebuild the fork entrance and its visual separation.** Make both road mouths continuous and legible before commitment. Remove the exposed loose-looking edge pieces and awkward ramp-to-sand overlap, give the elevated branch a convincing transition and supports, and use a substantial divider or terrain mass to separate the choices. Preserve simultaneous visibility of both options while giving the lower branch equal entrance clarity.
4. **Finish the inhabited and industrial architecture as connected places.** Grid and foundry need layered structures and meaningful mechanical joins: anchored footings, connected pipes, believable pipe terminations, access stairs/catwalk relationships, varied working bays, and material details scaled to each object. Add concentrated roadside occupation and equipment at the grid, and a purposeful finish landmark/retaining-wall composition at the finish. Repeating the same gate, tank, cone, and boulder cannot supply the target's environmental specificity.
