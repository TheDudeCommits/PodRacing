Exact pixel matching is not required. The common vehicle and camera difference is described separately rather than repeatedly subtracting the same vehicle mismatch from every world score.

## 01 Grid — 4.5/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The actual has a substantial trussed starting gantry, suspended colored lights, flanking sheds, orange cliffs, and a warm sky. Those establish the right broad place.

**Visible gap:** The target is a crowded, layered pit street with arched workshops, fabric roofs, stacked machinery, people, and equipment extending into depth. The actual presents a much emptier sandy corridor, a large foreground gantry, isolated workshop modules, and a distant open horizon. Large flat construction faces and clean silhouettes dominate. The left foreground concrete wall is especially visually blunt beside the target's inhabited workshop edge. Ground marks and material variation do not establish the target's worked, heavily used street.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Build dense workshop clusters immediately behind both sides of the gantry: varied arched entrances, deep interior shadow, worklights, parked machinery, stacked crates, hoses, and small human-scale silhouettes. Carry smaller clusters down the street so its depth is populated.
2. Add an irregular overhead canopy composition using several overlapping, visibly sagging fabric spans with poles, seams, ropes, and warm/cool color blocks. Break the gantry's uniform panels with mechanical junctions, cable bundles, chipped paint, and richer light housings.
3. Author the street surface and vista as one composition: longitudinal ruts, darker compacted racing lines, dusty shoulders and clustered debris, with layered mesas and settlement forms closing the currently empty far view.

## 02 Salt run — 3.5/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The actual has an open road between orange rock masses, a warm clouded sky, and some distant vertical formations.

**Visible gap:** The target's identity is an accelerating sweep through a long field of narrow, tilted, angular spires. The actual is a broad, largely straight sandy area flanked by a few bulky or upright forms, with a very open middle distance and a thin gate across the view. The target's layered silhouettes recede across the whole horizon; the actual terrain has far fewer distinct depth planes. The actual ground is comparatively uniform and the sky occupies much more of the composition.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Establish the target's terrain rhythm with a close sheer left wall, a prominent diagonally leaning right fin, and successively smaller, differently tilted spires following the road into the distance. Vary silhouettes deliberately instead of distributing more of the same rounded rock.
2. Shape a clearly visible sweeping road ribbon with directional worn bands, broken shoulder shelves, gravel transitions, and localized darker rock exposures. Let the road's visual flow carry the eye far beyond the foreground.
3. Add multiple distant mesa and spire layers with progressive color and contrast separation, then compose a more structured cloud ceiling. Integrate checkpoint furniture into the landscape so the thin repeated bar and cones do not become the main focal objects.

## 03 Canyon — 4.5/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The main actual image clearly shows a substantial natural-rock arch. Supplemental approach, under, and beyond views also show enclosing cliffs and the same narrow-canyon setting. The arch is not merely an object inferred from a label.

**Visible gap:** The target frames a rounded arch opening around a sunlit winding road and many staggered columns. Its walls have strong horizontal strata, eroded shelves, warm lit faces, cool crevices, and rubble transitions. The actual is dominated by thick, nearly uniform purple cliff faces with broad faceted surfaces and a taller, more irregular aperture. The supplemental views continue the same large vertical masses; they do not reveal the target's richly articulated, sunlit road vista. The exposed sand remains conspicuously simple against the cliffs.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Reshape and compose the arch as the principal geological landmark: a broader rounded aperture, layered overhanging crown, coherent bedding lines, broken lower shelves, and believable integration into both canyon walls.
2. Stage a sunlit road bend and staggered rock columns beyond the opening, using the arch's darker foreground to frame a distinct warm destination. Reduce the broad uniform purple value field through deliberately placed warm planes and deeper crevices.
3. Add a continuous erosion transition at the wall bases: talus fans, fractured ledges, angular boulders at several sizes, sand pockets, and worn road grooves. Replace isolated repeated small rock piles with clusters tied to specific cliff fractures.

## 04 Fork — 3.0/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The actual and supplemental views show an elevated branch on the left and a lower road sweeping alongside it. Cyan route markers and chevrons distinguish road edges. A visual route split exists.

**Visible gap:** The target is organized around one immense central rock pillar that physically separates a lower left shelf from a higher right cliff road. Its bridges and ledges feel carved into the canyon. The actual split sits in a wide, open sandy area: a smooth raised ramp runs left while a fenced lower road curves beside it. The central monumental obstruction and the deeply enclosed upper branch are absent. Clean curbs, repeating black chevron boards, and smooth ramp edges supply more of the identity than geology.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Give the split a dominant central rock mass at the decision point and stage both branch mouths clearly around it. The two routes need contrasting visible elevations and destinations in the same approach composition.
2. Integrate the upper branch into cliff shelves and rock-supported bridge spans, with visible underside structure, broken stone edges, and an enclosing cliff backdrop. Replace the isolated smooth ramp silhouette with a geographically grounded path.
3. Author distinct edge treatment for each branch: eroded stone parapets and cyan beacon housings on the upper shelf, broader sandy wear and rubble at the lower bend. Reduce the visual repetition of clean highway chevrons and uniform rails.

## 05 Launch — 3.5/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The actual shows a crest facing large mesas and an industrial landmark on the right. The supplemental descent image makes a descending road and lower basin visually apparent. This is stronger evidence than the main crest still alone.

**Visible gap:** The target reveals an enormous, richly populated basin with many isolated mesas, bridges, rock needles, hanging industrial structures, dust layers, and a clear winding route below. The actual view contains a small number of very large, smooth mesa masses, broad empty sand, a simple horizon, and a compact refinery silhouette. The main and crest shots devote much of the frame to sky and nearby road. Even the descent shot reveals a sparse basin, not the target's geographic spectacle.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Compose the crest reveal around visible vertical separation: near cliff edges, a legible lower road, intervening rock shelves, and distant basin terrain. Arrange the world and comparison viewpoint so the descent and depth are visible together.
2. Populate the basin with a deliberate hierarchy of isolated mesas, thin needles, arches, connecting ledges, and smaller geological fragments. Add several distant terrain layers with atmospheric separation instead of leaving large uninterrupted sand fields.
3. Enlarge and differentiate the industrial destination with domed tanks, multiple decks, suspended pipes, cross-canyon bridges, and smaller satellite structures anchored at different elevations. Add restrained dust or haze in the basin to separate those layers without obscuring them.

## 06 Foundry — 5.0/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The actual has large orange tanks, pale towers, interconnected pipes, ladders, platforms, and overhead spans. The supplemental images show that industrial scenery extends through several views. This is the strongest section-level thematic match.

**Visible gap:** The target is a dense, vertically layered industrial canyon whose varied towers, giant curved pipes, cranes, hanging equipment, and nested bridge spans establish a long street perspective. The actual has a visibly repeated tank/tower module with sparse secondary equipment, large clean surfaces, similar pipe diameters, and regular connections. Cyan accents are small flat strips rather than convincing concentrated light sources. The largely sandy, mounded road and sparse foundations disconnect the machinery from the richly worked ground in the target. The start-style light gantry also makes the main capture resemble another checkpoint rather than a unique foundry landmark.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Create a differentiated industrial skyline and street sequence: a foreground hero pipe elbow, tanks of distinct heights and silhouettes, intermediate crane bridges, and smaller distant spans. Avoid repeating the same tower-plus-tank pair along both sides.
2. Build substantial secondary and tertiary machinery around selected focal assemblies: flange rings, bolts, valves, braces, cable bundles, grilles, service catwalks, repair panels, hanging loads, and recessed blue-lit equipment. Use localized weathering, soot, rust, and paint loss to describe operation and material transitions.
3. Ground the installation with retaining walls, concrete and metal service aprons, footings, maintenance clutter, rubble berms, and dusty worn road bands. Compose a visible sequence of road openings through the machinery so foreground mounds and repeated pipe bars do not suppress the depth reveal.

## 07 Finish — 3.5/10 — strict FAIL

**Observed match:** The actual shows a bend around a cliff, repeated chevrons, a roadside structure, rails, and a rocky drop beyond the outside edge.

**Visible gap:** The target's main visual gesture is a banked, deeply worn cliff road, with a large close retaining wall, battered orange-white barriers, layered fences, signage hardware, and a finish gantry farther around the bend. The actual is a broad sandy corner with a clean low curb and uniform rail, simple flat chevron boards, a small blocky tower, and no comparably staged distant finish landmark in this capture. Large similar rock masses fill the background without the target's needle-like skyline or convincing cliff-road layering.

**Top three next changes:**

1. Author the bend as a strong banked road surface with continuous curved ruts, a substantial retaining wall on the inside, and a visibly worn racing line leading around the cliff. Give the road edge thickness and a clearly articulated drop below it.
2. Replace the clean barrier strip with varied weathered orange-white blocks, metal mounting brackets, layered fencing, lamps, and battered chevron assemblies. Use varied damage and material breakup concentrated where barriers meet the road and rock.
3. Place a distinctive finish gantry in the visible continuation of the bend and build a staggered spire skyline beyond the outside drop. Tie the roadside tower into a larger race-infrastructure cluster with platforms, cables, and lighting so it does not read as an isolated block.

## Largest gaps across all seven sections

1. **Scene-defining geometry and composition remain the largest gap.** The targets use a different authored spatial structure for every section: inhabited pit street, tilted-spire corridor, framed arch reveal, monumental fork pillar, vast basin reveal, layered industrial street, and banked cliff finish. Several actual views currently share the same broad sand, scattered large rocks, cones, and thin gates. More surface detail alone will not recover those identities.
2. **Geology needs distinct macroforms and coherent strata.** The actual mixes tall smooth mesa faces with very similar rough purple rock masses. The targets have shelves, broken sedimentary bands, angled fins, fractured bases, and many deliberate foreground-to-background silhouettes. Rock variety must change at the silhouette and landform level, not only through texture noise.
3. **The middle distance is underdeveloped.** Targets contain many nested layers between the nearest object and the horizon. Actual views often jump from large nearby objects to a few distant masses or open sky. Author smaller intervening terrain, structures, rubble, and road fragments, with diminishing contrast and scale.
4. **Materials are too even and construction too elementary.** The targets' painterly look depends on structured value and hue changes, broad chipped edges, small mechanical contrast, and localized wear. Actual sand, pipes, rails, and tanks remain dominated by broad simple surfaces. Adding indiscriminate noise would not solve this; wear needs to follow joints, edges, use, exposure, and scale.
5. **World detail and repeated race furniture need a shared visual language.** The target's functional details feel embedded in the environment. Actual cones, horizontal gate bars, chevron boards, and similar rock piles repeat across otherwise different sections. Design section-specific housings, supports, foundations, and neighboring clutter while preserving clear racing cues.
6. **Camera and foreground presentation remain far from the targets.** Every target uses a close, low chase framing with wide engine separation, a prominent cockpit/pilot, and dense visible machinery. Actual views look down at a much narrower central vehicle, leaving large uninterrupted road areas. This substantially changes scale and immediacy. A closer comparison framing would help assessment, but camera changes alone cannot repair the world gaps above. The vehicle's specific identity is outside these world scores; the visible geometry, material richness, light intensity, and cockpit presence are still separate full-frame parity gaps.

The most productive next pass is to resolve each section's first listed geometric/compositional change before applying a global material and detail pass. Grid and foundry already have recognizable architectural anchors; fork, launch, and salt run need the greatest change to their overall landform staging. None of the current still sets supports a strict target-art PASS.
