# Blind route-marker critique — round 30

Reviewed the ten A/B pairs individually, plus the three supplied target images. A is `round-30-light-before-markers`; B is `round-30-marker-candidate`. This assessment uses only these images. No source, project history, other critiques, browser, or running game was inspected.

**Verdict: B improves near-marker scale and reduces foreground clutter, but it is not an unqualified route-readability improvement. A retains more visible guidance at medium and far distances. Neither reaches the targets' overall art or route presentation.** I would use B's restrained near scale as a direction, while preserving stronger distant and crest/decision-point cues. I would not accept a uniform reduction as proven navigation-safe from these stills.

## Near and far scale

B's visible change is primarily a reduction of the cream-tipped dark poles. Nearby examples in the canyon, launch, and foundry frames are approximately half the former apparent height. That removes the impression of very tall, thin obelisks standing beside a small craft. It is a real compositional improvement, especially when A clips a huge marker at the screen edge.

The cost is not hypothetical: B's medium and far tips visibly occupy much less image area, and several cue sequences become difficult to pick out without comparing back to A. In the salt run, the route and off-route sand are similar in color and surface character, so the pole trail carries information the ground does not. In the canyon and launch views, smaller posts also disappear behind rising ground sooner. These are lost or weakened visual markings, not evidence that objects were removed from the implementation.

The cream tips contrast well against purple canyon walls, but less distinctly against bright sand and sky. The remaining dark tapered bases can merge with rocks and shadows. A physical height reduction alone does not address those conditions. A more useful direction would combine B-like nearby proportions with sufficient visible tip area, contrast, or additional route cues ahead of crests and branch entrances.

## Target comparison and parity

The salt-run target describes a long, continuous road through directional surface strokes, shaped shoulders, and geology arranged around the path. It does not rely on a line of posts. B is marginally closer in reduced marker intrusion, but neither candidate reproduces that naturally legible road ribbon; both still present broad similar-looking sand with gates and markers doing much of the guidance work.

The canyon target frames a clearly visible winding road through a substantial natural arch, with illuminated road surfaces and layered terrain carrying the eye deep into the scene. B lets the candidates' arch read more cleanly. The much flatter sand articulation and the obscured continuation remain major differences. Making the poles smaller does not create the target's depth, road texture, or terrain hierarchy.

The fork target presents two unmistakable branches around a central rock mass, with different elevations and continuous boundaries. Its turquoise edge lights reinforce geometry that is already clear. The candidates' raised ramp is visually distinct, but the lower branch is less explicit and is partly screened by that ramp. B makes the turquoise-versus-cream cue hierarchy more apparent by reducing the cream poles; that may also bias attention toward the upper route. Neither candidate achieves the target's balanced visibility of both branch entrances.

Overall parity is **not achieved**. The candidates have a coherent warm desert/purple-shadow palette, but the targets retain substantially richer rock strata, ground directionality, layered depth, material detail, and vehicle presence. B is a local scale adjustment, not a broad move into the target's visual quality. Its clearest value is reducing oversized nearby signage; its clearest risk is losing advance markings where terrain alone does not define the road.

The displayed speed value does not establish that either route system works at that speed. These stills cannot prove recognition time, camera-motion legibility, avoidance behavior, branch choice, or successful traversal. Those remain untested by this review.
