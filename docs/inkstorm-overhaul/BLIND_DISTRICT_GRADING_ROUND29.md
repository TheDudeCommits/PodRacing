The canopy has a useful sagging silhouette, but its large angular planes and thin posts remain simpler than the concept's layered fabric and industrial attachments. I cannot establish a geometric hover from this frame, but stronger local contact would help. A's foreground dune supplies more organic relief, yet it does so by obscuring the actual work zone; that is not a useful quality to preserve. Retain B and introduce restrained terrain variation outside the cleared approach.

## Settlement coherence and concept parity

B is a meaningful grounding correction. The district buildings now read as workshops placed on usable slabs with visible equipment, rather than buildings and props accidentally overtaken by terrain. This addresses a foundational scene problem.

The concept still has much stronger physical and social density: heavy curved hangars, layered gantries, visibly joined salvage structures, crowded work clusters, varied human silhouettes, grounded machinery, worn dusty approaches, and fabric with finer folds and attachment logic. These diagnostic images instead show large plain walls, repeated shallow shelf details, thin poles, broad angular fabric, widely spaced props, and small block-like figures. The warm canyon palette and blue/orange materials connect the scenes, but do not establish strict visual parity. The concept's route composition and foreground vehicle cannot be evaluated fairly from these cropped diagnostic views.

## Strongest next action

**Keep B's cleared district work zones, then finish one continuous ground-to-workshop approach before adding more scenery.** Retain level interior slabs; replace abrupt frontage cuts and the remaining pit banks with believable short transitions, ramps, or explicitly supported retaining edges. Blend earth into the edges with shallow, irregular compacted sand, wheel wear, and local contact darkening while keeping entrances, stand runners, trolley wheels, and canopy feet exposed. Validate this at a low oblique angle as well as these diagnostic views. The desired result is machinery and people sharing a convincingly usable surface, without reintroducing dunes through the working areas.

After that, the largest concept gap is richer constructed workshop detail and varied occupied work clusters. Terrain grading alone does not close it.
