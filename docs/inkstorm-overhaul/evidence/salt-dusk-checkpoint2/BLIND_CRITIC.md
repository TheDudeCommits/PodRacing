# Independent visual critique — combined-pass7

**Verdict: owner-reviewable progress Preview; not the final visual target.**

**Aesthetic quality: 6/10. Approved-concept match: 4/10.** These are visual judgments against the supplied photoreal salt-flats concept, not engineering or performance scores.

The current game has a coherent blue-and-ochre dusk palette, a recognizable racing vehicle, a clean selection screen, and enough variation in its environments to make the direction reviewable. It still reads as a moderately detailed real-time game scene beneath a much more realistic sky. The approved concept reads as a continuous photographed world: geology, ground, machinery, atmosphere, and speed all support one another. That integration is the remaining target.

## Evidence and scope

I inspected the approved `03-salt-flats.png` and the actual `00-menu.png`, `00-portrait.png`, `00-pod-1.png`, `00-pod-2.png`, `00-pod-3.png`, `01-grid.png`, `02-salt-run.png`, `02b-salt-basin.png`, `03-canyon.png`, `04-fork.png`, `05-launch.png`, `06-foundry.png`, and `07-finish.png` directly as images. I did not read implementation documents, prior critiques, receipts, or test scores. I did not run the game or a browser.

These diagnostic stills cannot establish native frame rate, animation quality, camera feel, gameplay, or the actual strength of effects during ordinary racing. Where effects are absent from the images, this report describes the visible presentation and does not assume those systems are absent from the implementation.

## What works

- **The menu is coherent and readable.** Navy framing, restrained amber accents, a strong vehicle name, and a prominent Race action make `00-menu.png` feel like a deliberate product screen. The portrait composition retains the full vehicle and legible controls without visible overlap.
- **The vehicle is a useful focal point.** Its paired engine silhouette, cables, worn blue/orange paint, and cockpit establish the premise immediately. The other conventional pods have substantially different silhouettes; Sebulba has particularly useful exposed mechanical detail.
- **The light establishes a setting.** The low sun, warm edges, cool shadows, and cloud color work together in the basin and launch views. The foundry silhouette on a distant bluff gives the route a destination.
- **There are distinct spatial moments.** The canyon arch frames a passage effectively. The launch vista opens the scale up. The foundry provides dense overhead structures after the open basin. That sequence has visual potential.
- **Primary HUD numbers are easy to find.** The center of the screen remains mostly available for the world, and the small cyan energy connection is restrained.

## Ranked visible defects and concrete changes

1. **The ground does not yet sell a salt flat.** In `02-salt-run.png`, `02b-salt-basin.png`, and `05-launch.png`, the surface is covered by broad, soft rolling mounds and diffuse golden patches. It reads more like sand dunes, with fine crack texture laid over them. The concept has a vast, predominantly level crust with broken reflective streaks, scoured racing paths, and small-scale irregularities. Flatten the main basin and driving corridor substantially; reserve large drifts for margins and lee sides. Build the visual interest from salt crust variation, irregular wet/dry roughness, low ridges, and directional worn paths. This is the single largest concept-fidelity change.

2. **Large geology exposes its simplified construction.** The massive left wall in the salt run and the mesas in the launch view have smooth, tapering, nearly uniform silhouettes. Their evenly distributed surface detail does not describe sediment layers or erosion at the scale of the landform. Some canyon joins also read as large intersecting slabs. Add broad stratification, varied ledges, broken shoulders, undercuts, talus skirts, and medium-sized rock families at their bases. Prioritize the skyline and near/middle-distance formations seen from the route over more tiny texture noise. The canyon arch is the strongest starting point; the launch mesas need the most silhouette work.

3. **The images do not convey the concept's race energy or ground contact.** The HUD shows roughly 410 KPH, but these stills show a crisp isolated vehicle, almost no visible trailing salt/dust, weak white exhaust cores, and no readable field of nearby rivals. The concept's blue jets, layered dust wakes, rival silhouettes, and directional ground movement carry much of its excitement. Supply an actual racing view with rivals, sustained exhaust, light contact dust, and directional motion cues before judging this dimension complete. Keep the vehicle readable and let particles catch the low sun; a generic full-screen blur would not replace these cues. I cannot infer from this gallery whether these effects are missing in ordinary play.

4. **The high-quality sky is not yet matched by material and lighting richness below it.** The foundry has useful structural complexity, but pipes and beams often read as simple colored forms with broadly repeated wear. Deeply shaded vehicle panels lose their detail, while some lit rock surfaces become uniformly yellow. The concept has much more varied metal response, localized abrasion, dust accumulation, fractured rock shading, and atmospheric separation. Improve surface-specific roughness and edge response, place weathering where water/dust/contact would collect, and preserve readable bounced light in important shadows. The goal is consistent realism across the frame, not extra saturation or bloom.

5. **The route furniture and repeated obstacles feel placed on a level, rather than embedded in a landscape.** The basin repeatedly uses similar isolated rock stacks and skinny posts. Fork/finish signs are large black rectangles with simple amber chevrons; guardrails and ramps have very clean continuous edges. Compared with the concept's subtle edge lights and naturally scattered rocks, these elements make the scene feel constructed. Keep necessary navigational clarity, but vary rock size/orientation, form clusters and debris skirts, partially bury foundations, weather road edges, and give the racing infrastructure a consistent material family. Avoid merely adding more of the same evenly spaced objects.

6. **Presentation polish is uneven at the edges.** The gameplay cockpit nearly meets or clips the lower frame, making the hero feel cramped in several views. Secondary HUD copy such as Route, Shift/Redline, and small resource labels loses contrast over bright ground or sky. The menu's very large empty upper showcase area and thin outline controls are tidy but still somewhat generic. Give the gameplay vehicle a little bottom clearance, strengthen secondary-label contrast without adding bulky panels, and refine the menu lighting so each pod has a strong three-quarter hero pose with visible surfaces. `00-pod-2.png` is especially dark and nearly side-on. Blockrunner's studded toy construction is a deliberate-looking stylistic outlier: it may be a valid novelty vehicle, but it does not support the approved photoreal direction and should not be the evidence used to claim that direction is complete.

## Concept match and next review

The concept's **premise** is present: paired engines, exposed cockpit, a mineral desert, low warm light, rock landmarks, industrial structures, and a sparse racing HUD. Its **image-making** is not yet present at the same level: a broad flat sweep through the landscape, complex eroded rock, reflective scuffed crust, dust-lit rivals, convincing exhaust, and a continuous photographic material response.

For the next visual checkpoint, concentrate effort on one representative basin race view: a flatter salt surface, a more eroded landmark, a visible pack of rivals and dust wakes, strong but controlled blue exhaust, and refined ground/metal response under the current sunset. Then judge the canyon and foundry against that established quality level. The current menu, silhouette variety, and route landmarks justify showing the owner an honest progress Preview now. These renders do not justify calling the photoreal target achieved or the final look approved.

## Addendum — native Battle stills

This separate addendum preserves the original critique and its scores: **aesthetic quality 6/10; concept match 4/10; owner-reviewable progress Preview, not the final visual target.** I directly inspected `native-pass7/battle-pack.png`, `battle-straight.png`, `battle-sector-3.png`, `battle-sector-5.png`, and `battle-sector-7.png`. The capture was supplied as ordinary eight-racer Battle play through virtual-gamepad input. I did not independently replay it. I consulted only the adjacent JSON's player recovery/shield state to avoid mistaking an exceptional state for ordinary presentation; no performance measurements informed this assessment.

**Rivals are now visibly established.** The straight shows several other vehicles ahead, sector 5 shows distinct racers entering the foundry, and sector 7 shows a racer with twin trails. The gallery-only concern that the racing presentation had no readable competitors is therefore partly resolved by this evidence. These frames establish a populated race more convincingly than the diagnostic gallery. They still do not show the concept's broad composition of several racers at different distances with substantial overlapping dust wakes.

**Dust exists, but its visible contribution is modest.** Sector 7 provides the clearest evidence: a rival leaves two narrow, pale brown trails that help connect it to the ground and indicate travel direction. The trails are thin and relatively even; they do not yet build the broken, sunlit, spreading particulate volumes that make the approved concept feel fast and physical. The straight and sector 5 remain visually quite clean around the racers. Prioritize a dense emitter near the contact zone that expands and dissipates irregularly, with low-sun illumination and enough transparency to preserve the route. This is refinement of a visible effect, not a claim that dust is absent.

**Player exhaust remains weak in these images.** `battle-pack.png` shows short white plumes; the rear-facing race views mostly show pale white nozzle centers with little visible extension. The compact cyan cable connection is clear. I still do not see the strong blue-white jet shape or lit dust interaction of the concept. The pack and straight JSON identify recovery states, so those two frames alone cannot establish normal full-throttle exhaust quality. Across the remaining supplied stills, however, there is still no image evidence that closes this visual gap. A normal sustained-throttle or boost frame should show the intended plume shape before this item is considered resolved.

**Combat adds visible activity, but the effect style is less realistic than the target.** Orange weapon streaks and scattered fragments give sector 7 and the straight more action. The cyan/green translucent volumes in pack, straight, and sector 3 are visually dominant, with flat polygon faces and conspicuous outlines. They read as game indicators more than luminous energy interacting with the world. Sector 3's large cyan volume covers much of the foreground and reduces vehicle readability. Whatever their precise gameplay state, the visible treatment would benefit from lower interior opacity, a finer edge response, and more localized light or impact detail. Keep the useful immediacy of the combat cues without allowing large flat tinted surfaces to dominate the hero.

The native stills substantiate rivals, some dust, short exhaust, and combat effects. They improve the evidence for race activity; they do not close the visual gap to the approved concept. Terrain, geology, material integration, and presentation concerns remain as written above. No inference about motion smoothness, animation quality, frame rate, or input feel can be drawn from these stills.
