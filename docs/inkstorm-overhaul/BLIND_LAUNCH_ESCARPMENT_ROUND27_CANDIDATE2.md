## Focused observations

**Launch:** The paired cliff masses successfully frame an open central passage. B's left silhouette is calmer than A's. However, the left wall's broad rounded cap and long parallel shaded strips read more like a fluted extrusion than successive fractured rock faces. On the right, the cliff below the industrial structures has a similarly continuous, soft upper rim. The focused target achieves its scale through offset blocks, broken ledges, and dark recesses distributed across the whole face; B concentrates that complexity in a few smaller foreground pieces.

**Crest:** This angle makes the assembly problem especially clear. The two squat orange stacks at the roadside appear placed on a smooth plane, and the sharp, highly textured rock pieces sit in front of substantially smoother escarpments. There is insufficient transition in geometry, texture scale, and debris to make them read as remnants of the same eroding formation. The central route remains readable, though its far continuation relies heavily on the gate and posts rather than a strong sequence of terrain shelves.

**Descent:** The removal of A's tall central needle is an improvement. B's dominant wall nevertheless remains a nearly uninterrupted slab from its rounded top to its flared foot. The detailed orange formation against its lower right face appears planted in front of that slab. The distant basin has a long, comparatively uniform rim and sparse intermediate relief, so the immense layered distance of the original target is still missing.

**Pincushion / rounded-wall / planted-scan check:** The scene is not predominantly a pincushion field, and B improves the worst local needle. A few thin fins remain around cliff tops. The dominant issue is now broad rounded walls, with separate detailed rock pieces that visually resemble planted scans. This is an appearance judgment, not a claim about how the assets were produced.

## Top three fixes

1. **Cut deep, angular bays into the two hero escarpments.** Break the left slab and the right industrial wall into several unequal buttresses with genuinely recessed backs, offset face directions, broken caps, and intermediate shelves. Replace selected soft vertical grooves with large stepped cavities whose shadow and silhouette read from launch and descent. Preserve the central passage and the visible left turn. More surface crack lines alone will not provide this scale of form.
2. **Join the detailed rock pieces to a shared geological base.** Embed their feet into uneven talus aprons; carry matching ledges and fractured blocks between the detailed pieces and the broad walls; break the straight wall-to-sand junction with partially buried rubble and sediment fans. Replace the isolated crest stacks with asymmetric clusters connected to nearby outcrops. Match the transition pieces' roughness, color, and fracture scale so the assembled terrain reads as one formation.
3. **Build intermediate depth while unifying material scale.** Give the distant basin rim an uneven terraced profile and add a small number of overlapping low shelves outside the road, with decreasing contrast into the distance. Across the hero walls, use broad strata, localized dark fracture interiors, and directional weathering tied to ledges; reduce the uniformly thin crack pattern spanning giant smooth surfaces. Keep the road visually quieter and its boundary continuous so added depth does not compete with the route.

An 8/10 result needs the new bays, shared bases, and intermediate shelves to remain convincing across all three angles. The current improvement is worth retaining, but it is not terrain acceptance.
