Overall scores weight the section's defining visual purpose; they are not simple averages of the six columns.

### 01 Grid

The gantry creates a recognizable entrance, the racer separates well from the road, and there are structures on both sides. The concept's inhabited pit settlement is missing from the visual impression. The foreground gantry is extremely large and simple, while the pit buildings become thin, distant side dressing. Empty sand banks and a broad uninterrupted sky dominate the middle of the frame. The scene contains objects, but does not yet communicate a busy, built-up racing place.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Bring varied hangar mouths, work bays, fabric awnings, cables, and clustered equipment into the near shoulders; use several asymmetric clusters instead of uniformly repeated small props.
- Give the gantry mechanical joints, layered plates, contrasting worn edges, and attached equipment so its large screen footprint earns attention.
- Add a darker worn road ribbon with directional grooves and embedded dust patches, leaving brighter sand toward the shoulders. Preserve the central driving corridor.

### 02 Salt run

The concept makes a fast open run feel immense through irregular leaning rock blades, many depth layers, and directional ground treatment. The capture presents a broad smooth sand bowl, a few upright isolated rock masses, a distant simple industrial landmark, and thin marker poles. The sky occupies a large fraction of the image without a comparably strong ground composition. The small similar roadside mounds visibly repeat.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Add a rhythm of near broken ledges, mid-distance tilted fins, and smaller hazed spires that leads toward the road's vanishing point.
- Vary rock silhouettes and placement; avoid a field of similarly round, vertically striped masses.
- Replace the uniformly bright floor with broad, lane-aligned wear and shallow channels. Increase texture and object scale cues toward the camera, not evenly across the image.

### 03 Canyon

The arch and enclosing walls give this section one of the clearest location identities. The bright opening beyond the dark enclosure works. However, the main walls read as large smooth extrusions covered in regular pale stripes. The arch opening is abruptly jagged, with little believable ledge or rubble progression around its base. The concept's winding road continues visibly through a layered canyon; here a crest and tight opening truncate that depth. A hazard label is visible, but the hazardous physical feature is not obvious from this image.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Shape the opening with asymmetric strata, a believable overhang thickness, and irregular broken shelves; dress the wall-foot transition with grouped rubble.
- Introduce large cool shadow planes, smaller warm light-catching ledges, and selective dark cracks. Avoid uniform dark burgundy walls with evenly spaced pale lines.
- Expose more of the road beyond the arch so the eye can follow a bend through at least two depth layers.

### 04 Fork

The raised path and signed lower hairpin are visible, but this is not the concept's immediately understandable split around a central rock landmark. The raised branch reads as a manufactured ramp laid over a sand surface. Its entry has thin exposed slab edges and overlapping-looking ground joins. The second path is largely inferred from chevrons and distant railings rather than read as a continuous branch at the choice point. Repeated chevrons supply navigation but do not supply the missing terrain composition.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Re-stage the approach so both traversable branches are visibly continuous from the same foreground decision area.
- Give the split a substantial central rock/divider and make the high branch hug or traverse a landform with clear structural support.
- Resolve the ramp mouth into a coherent transition with a consistent width and edge treatment. The narrow exposed edges currently make the route look assembled from separate plates.
- Use cyan route beacons as a clear sequence along the alternative route, with geometry still doing most of the explanatory work.

### 05 Launch

The concept's defining event is a broad elevated reveal: the road drops into a large valley, rock shelves recede far below, and the industrial destination anchors the far side. In the capture, two huge central rock masses block most of that reveal. The visible road falls out of view into a narrow opening. There is a distant factory silhouette, but it sits on the obstructing mass rather than completing a layered panorama. The image reads as another ridge into a corridor.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Open or relocate the middle-distance blocking masses so the launch crest reveals a long descending road and several terraces below.
- Give the near sides a strong height cue: a cut bank, ledge, or drop whose scale contrasts with the road far beneath it.
- Place the industrial landmark beyond the valley, with multiple smaller structures and a clear foreground-to-background progression.
- Unify smooth terrain slopes and craggy rock inserts. Their different surface treatment is conspicuous here.

### 06 Foundry

The tank-lined corridor is recognizable, and it has more environmental enclosure than the open sections. The repeated simple cylinders, blue bands, sparse pipes, and single dominant gantry still feel like the first layer of an industrial scene. The concept gains depth from overlapping overhead pipes, crosswalks, suspended equipment, varied towers, dense connections, and strongly separated light/shadow masses. Those layers are mostly absent. The road also rises into a mound that hides the next spatial beat.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Add several overhead pipe/catwalk layers at different depths and heights; use one closer crossing to frame the view and a smaller second crossing to pull the eye onward.
- Vary tank dimensions, grouping, supporting platforms, fittings, and silhouette attachments. Concentrate detail at plausible joints, valves, bases, and service areas.
- Develop panel-value variation, edge wear, flange depth, concentrated grime, and selective cyan industrial lights. Uniform red cylinders with blue rings are too diagrammatic at this scale.
- Let the route visibly continue into the factory, avoiding an immediate sand mound that terminates the vista.

### 07 Finish

The left turn is readable because of the railing and repeated chevrons. The cliffside setting is present. It lacks the concept's feeling of a fast engineered road cut into a dramatic rock face: there is little banking, little substantial roadside wall detail, no convincing deep drop in the principal view, and no strong overhead destination/finish structure. The isolated central tower and rock mounds make the turn feel sparsely decorated.

Highest-impact fixes:

- Develop the outside edge into a clearly elevated route with visible cliff depth, and make the inside wall/road bank a strong continuous shape.
- Replace thin generic railings and clean signboards with a coherent, weathered barrier system whose scale and joints are visible near the camera.
- Put a distinctive finish/destination structure into the turn's sightline and support it with near, middle, and far rock layers.

## Shared priorities

1. **Fix the launch and fork compositions.** These are defining route moments, and their current shapes do not communicate the concepts' intent. More surface texture cannot compensate for a hidden valley or an ambiguous branch mouth.
2. **Replace uniform stripes with authored geology.** Pale horizontal bands have similar spacing and width across many rock families. Break their continuity, width, contrast, and alignment; use broad plane colors, cracks, chipped ledges, and irregular silhouette changes. The smoothly striped terrain and craggy rock inserts need a unified visual language.
3. **Make the road a physical, readable surface.** Add directional worn channels, broad value variation, shoulder accumulation, and embedded stones. Keep the racing line readable. Currently the road, drivable-looking shoulders, and sand slopes often share almost the same value and finish.
4. **Build depth through staged clusters.** Near ledges and equipment should frame the player, mid-distance structures should shape the road, and smaller hazed masses should establish scale. More isolated bollards or evenly spaced identical mounds will not achieve the concepts' density.
5. **Give large industrial forms enough detail for their screen size.** Tanks, gantries, pipes, and pit buildings need mechanical joints, contrasting panels, supports, and layered connections. Concentrate detail where it explains construction rather than sprinkling generic noise.
6. **Preserve the readable racer while improving its finish.** Its cyan engine rings, black hose curves, blue body, and orange accents are strong. The concept's richer panel hierarchy, mechanical recesses, and purposeful wear remain ahead of the current relatively broad clean surfaces. This is secondary to the larger world gaps.

The images support calling this a coherent stylized racing scene with a presentable garage. They do not support calling the world matched to the supplied concept quality yet.
