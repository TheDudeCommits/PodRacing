Strict concept parity fails for both candidates.** B gives several machines and work areas a more deliberate warm/cool hierarchy, but it does not turn these sparse, simplified bays into the materially rich and visibly inhabited workshops shown in the targets. The workshop target uses warm light as an explanation of the space: visible illuminated fixtures, concentrated highlights on machine edges, warm floor patches, and readable workers at actual workstations. Cool blue shells and equipment retain texture, seams, and reflected light. B moves toward that relationship through selective warmer highlights, especially on the right engine, but its pole lamps still read as dull solid blocks rather than clear light sources. The bays do not yet show the target's connected pattern of warm fixtures and illuminated activity.

Machinery is easier to locate in B, especially the right engine. Its silhouette, however, remains a small set of clean cylinders and rings. The left fan, hoist beams, crates, and cabinets also remain visibly simple. The target establishes weight and function through layered rims, fasteners, hoses, suspension hardware, workbench contents, and dense but legible contact details. Neither candidate reaches that level of construction realism. Stronger contrast cannot supply those missing forms.

Crew are a particularly large gap. The candidates contain tiny upright blocklike figures, but I cannot clearly read their tools, hand placement, posture, or task from these images. In the workshop target, people are large enough and locally lit enough to explain equipment scale and workshop activity. The candidate figures still feel incidental to the scene. B neither clearly improves nor clearly worsens their readability overall.

The broad composition tracks the workshop target: two curved bays, blue and orange overhead fabric, engine hoists, cases, and canyon surroundings. The original grid target additionally conveys a dense, worn industrial settlement through layered structural members, patched surfaces, visible crews, and many distinct working objects. Both candidates retain large empty interior surfaces, repeated simple props, uniformly straight supports, and very sparse mechanical dressing. Their material wear is mostly faint surface marking rather than the targets' convincing layered construction. The bright linear marks across the lower and side areas are also visually distracting in these stills; their cause or behavior is not assessed.

**Detail-loss finding:** B does lose visible upper-interior definition compared with A. This is not a wholesale machinery readability regression: the selective equipment highlights generally outweigh it, which is why B wins the pairs. But the darker vaults are not themselves evidence of greater realism. Preserve or restore enough reflected light and material variation to describe the curved shells while keeping the new equipment separation.

**Recommended direction:** keep B's selective warm highlights, restore subtle readable structure in the dark vaults, and establish visible work-light sources with corresponding illumination on crew, main machinery, and floor. Increase functional detail in the shell, hoist, and engine construction, and make the workers legibly engaged with equipment. These are necessary visual changes before another strict parity claim; B is an incremental contrast improvement, not a concept-parity pass.
