# Blind launch escarpment review — round 27 candidate 1

**Decision: retain B27 as the working terrain base; do not accept it as target quality.** My terrain assessment is **A26: 5.5/10; B27: 6.1/10**, against a strict **8/10** threshold. The new cuts improve the large shapes, especially in the descent view, but they are an intermediate blockout improvement. Even with the sky halo disregarded, B27 does not have the believable fractured cliffs, layered depth, or spectacle of either target.

This is an image-only comparison. I directly viewed the following eight images with the image viewer, without reading source code, previous reviews, or handover claims:

- Original visual target: `docs/inkstorm-overhaul/concepts/05-launch.png`.

These are subjective art scores, not calculated performance measurements; the overall score is a holistic judgment.

**Launch:** B27's left cliff has a more varied crown and a useful inset shelf. On the right, the lowered outer rim reveals more separation around the industrial shelf. Those are improvements. However, the added pointed slots and sharp triangular peaks look cut into a smooth shell rather than formed by fractured rock. The enormous faces still carry most of their detail as thin surface lines.

**Crest:** The silhouette improvement persists from a second angle, which supports retaining the change. The right-hand cliff now has more distinct levels, but its top also has a conspicuous sawtooth rhythm. The broad face below stays very smooth. The two isolated foreground rocks and detailed spires still feel placed in front of the terrain rather than produced by the same erosion system.

**Descent:** This is the strongest B27 improvement. The central wall now has a setback, a projecting shelf, and different upper heights instead of one continuous domed crown. It also exposes the remaining problem most clearly: the middle recess is a very tall pointed wedge, the shelf resembles a clean horizontal extrusion, and the massive wall transitions abruptly into the sandy floor. More contour variation alone will not solve those relationships.

## Distance from the targets

The focused paintover is the fairest direct terrain comparison because it preserves the same basic route and industrial landmark. Its cliffs have broad rectangular fracture blocks, short broken ledges, irregular buttresses, and rubble that visibly connects wall to valley floor. The dark recesses have width and internal structure. B27 has begun adding large cuts but is still missing that medium scale of construction. Surface cracks cannot substitute for it.

The original launch concept sets a higher composition benchmark: a foreground overhang, staggered towers and shelves through the basin, atmospheric separation, and a distant settlement integrated across multiple rock platforms. B27 retains a strong canyon gateway, but neither A26 nor B27 yet delivers that long landscape reveal. That is a terrain/depth gap independent of the sky artifact.

## Three highest-value fixes

1. **Replace the pointed cuts with broad, irregular fracture bays.** Prioritize the central wall in `launch-descent` and the left wall in `05-launch`. Widen selected recesses, offset their floors and back walls, and interrupt their vertical runs with two or three substantial broken shelves. Vary bay width, setback, and termination height. Break the right skyline into a few heavy stepped blocks rather than evenly legible teeth. The acceptance cue is shaded side planes and visible shelf thickness, not just a darker triangular patch.

2. **Add the missing medium rock masses and connect them to the floor.** Build a small number of substantial buttresses and fractured bench clusters between the smooth wall faces and the existing craggy spires. Carry selected buttresses downward into sloping talus aprons with a few larger fallen slabs. Break the near-straight wall/floor junction, particularly beneath the descent wall and the right industrial escarpment. Preserve the marked driving corridor. The acceptance cue is one coherent rock formation from crown to rubble toe rather than separate detail objects in front of a wall.

3. **Make the cliff material follow the new geology.** Reduce the uniform thin crack network on broad surfaces; establish a few readable sediment bands, sharper broken ledge edges, and larger exposed planes before adding small cracks. Use restrained warm/cool and roughness variation between dusty tops, exposed faces, and sheltered recesses. Match the main walls and craggy spires in detail scale and contrast. Keep structural shadow boundaries tied to actual ledges and setbacks; painting more dark lines onto the current smooth shell will not reach the paintover.

## Separate postprocessing observation

B27 shows a conspicuous pale/orange outline beyond cliff silhouettes, particularly around the descent wall's crown and outer right edge. The sky also appears darker than A26, so the pair is not visually identical outside the terrain. The outline reduces finish quality and makes edges harder to judge. **It is excluded from the terrain score and from the three terrain fixes above.** Removing it alone would not raise this terrain to 8/10.

No FPS, frame pacing, motion quality, collision, or runtime stability conclusion can be drawn from these stills.
