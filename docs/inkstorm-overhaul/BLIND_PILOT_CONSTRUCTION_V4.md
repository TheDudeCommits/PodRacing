# Blind pilot construction review — V4

Image-only review of the side, front-quarter, and full renders for V4 and V3, compared with `pilot-material-guide.png` and `teemto-inkstorm.png`. No source, handover, other critic reports, browser, or Blender was consulted.

**Verdict: retain V4 as the working candidate, but do not accept it. Current: 6.1/10. Prior V3: 5.8/10. Required bar: 8/10.**

V4 makes the pilot less pristine and more compatible with the weathered vehicle. The fabric, helmet grime, and elimination of the conspicuous orange wrist sliver are useful gains. Those gains are partly offset by the oversized angular collar, irregular near cuff, and rough garment panel boundaries. Neither candidate reaches the targets' convincing sewn construction, material separation, or authored close-up finish.

## Frame-visible defects and ambiguity

- **Patch: looks unintentionally split or clipped.** In both V4 close views, a dark oblique line crosses the lower portion of the orange upper-arm patch and its silhouette becomes a small angled lower segment. There is no clear stitched border or deliberate graphic explaining that break. A still cannot establish actual mesh intersection, but the rendered appearance is not a clean intentional patch. V3's rectangle is plainer but visually cleaner.
- **Shoulders: look unfinished.** The near shoulder cap has a hard, irregular boundary and weak connection to the torso. Around the collar and harness, thin angular edges read as stacked sheets. The new texture does not establish which edges are seams, folds, or separate layers. This is visible ambiguity rather than proof of a specific topology error.
- **Cuffs: the near cuff looks unintentional; the far cuff looks deliberate but crude.** The near wrist has an uneven raised polygonal lip that rides over the glove. The far wrist reads as a thick smooth rubber ring. Their mismatched treatment breaks the sense of one suit. V4 improves wrist coverage, but not the quality of the joint.
- **Collar: contact improves, silhouette regresses.** V4's collar conceals the bare cylindrical neck but rises into a broad pointed shape under the helmet. It suggests rigid folded sheet material. The material guide's compact dark ribbed neck seal is more convincing and preserves separation between helmet and suit.
- **Other small distractions:** a triangular protrusion is visible around the near elbow/forearm transition; a small bright triangular area appears at the lap in the V4 side view; tiny isolated flecks remain just to the upper right of the helmet in the front-quarter view. These should be checked as rendered artifacts, not presumed intentional wear.

## Three fixes, in priority order

1. **Resolve garment boundaries before adding more texture.** Make the near shoulder, elbow, forearm, and cuff form a continuous garment with controlled panel transitions. Remove visible triangular protrusions and stray bright fragments. Make the orange patch one clearly attached surface with a continuous border. It must look intentional in both close views.
2. **Rebuild the neck and wrist interfaces.** Use a compact dark neck seal with readable layering beneath the helmet, reduce the large pointed collar, and give both wrists consistent cuffs that compress around the gloves. Preserve the improved wrist coverage and seated grip, then add glove panel and knuckle definition at an appropriate scale.
3. **Tune materials around construction and readable accents.** Reduce the coarse uniform weave, place wear at seams and contact edges, and distinguish cloth, webbing, glove leather/rubber, painted helmet, and buckle metal. Give the patch and buckles precise edges and enough contrast to survive a medium view. The targets' advantage comes from coherent construction and restrained detail, not simply more dirt.

The full renders provide no meaningful visible pilot improvement at this framing: the cockpit is too small to judge garment work. They support broad palette and vehicle silhouette comparison only. Differences from the generated whole-vehicle target are also affected by camera position and framing. These still images establish no runtime, animation, collision, FPS, or deployment result.
