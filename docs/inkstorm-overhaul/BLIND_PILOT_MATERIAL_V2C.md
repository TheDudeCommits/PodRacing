The target gap is substantial: its pilot has layered fabric, compact protective equipment, convincing webbing and localized wear, all integrated into a dense mechanical cockpit. This candidate preserves the broad palette and seated-control idea, but close views expose simplified construction. In the full render the cockpit is too small to assess these details; that image cannot compensate for the close-up weaknesses. Lighting and framing differ from the target, so surface-response comparisons are qualitative.

## Top three fixes

1. **Give the suit and harness believable construction.** Shape elbow, wrist and waist compression; add restrained seam and reinforcement structure; give straps visible thickness, woven response, tension and readable lap anchoring. This is the largest source of the mannequin impression.
2. **Finish the helmet and gloves as distinct manufactured objects.** Articulate brow/jaw/ear components and glove cuffs/knuckles, vary roughness locally, and add selective paint wear and crease wear. Reduce the neck stack's glossy prominence. Preserve the currently readable finger wrap while improving finger and handle detail.
3. **Recompose the cockpit around the pilot's face and hands.** Reduce or redirect visually dominant overhead/chest hoses, temper the bright foreground rods, and give the control area a darker layered mechanical backdrop. Keep the seat bolsters and hand positions readable rather than obscuring them with added detail.
