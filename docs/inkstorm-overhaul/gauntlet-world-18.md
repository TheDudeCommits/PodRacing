## Cross-section priorities

1. **Compose the terrain before adding more small props.** The salt run, fork, launch, and finish need connected landforms and routes that remain visible across multiple depths. The concepts devote the frame to substantial shaped terrain; the captures often devote it to empty sky and sand around disconnected rocks.
2. **Unify the rock language.** Tall smooth polygonal pillars, visibly gridded surfaces, dark angular slabs, and pale crumpled rock clusters currently sit beside one another without a consistent geological treatment. Establish a recognizable family of stratified mesas, fins, shelves, and eroded bases with shared scale and color variation.
3. **Bring environmental materials up to the vehicle's level of articulation.** The racer has panel divisions, cables, accents, and chips; the world often consists of large uninterrupted cylinders, beams, walls, and ramps. Add meaningful seams, joints, flanges, wear concentrations, and base connections. Avoid distributing equal noise everywhere; emphasize close and focal structures.
4. **Use lighting to separate planes.** The warm palette is coherent, but the overall fill is high and the distance often becomes uniformly pale. The concepts have darker cool foreground shadows, strong warm lit faces, and several distinct depth layers. The canyon already benefits from an enclosed dark frame and is the clearest direction to develop.
5. **Give each industrial landmark its own design.** The starting gantry is a clear anchor. Repeating its hanging-disc treatment in the foundry dilutes that identity. The factory vista needs smaller supporting infrastructure and a believable connection to its terrain; the foundry needs a specifically industrial overhead composition.
6. **Enrich the road surface and edge integration.** The captures have a brown road strip and some texture, but little large-scale directional wear, compacted grooves, broken shoulders, or accumulated debris. The concepts use those features both for material credibility and to lead the eye. Repeated thin black-and-cream markers and small similar boulders cannot carry this environmental work alone.
