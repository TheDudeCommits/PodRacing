## Visible contacts, overlaps, and gaps

- **Helmet versus overhead hose:** In the front-quarter image, the thick black hose crosses the upper-left helmet crown in projection and masks part of its silhouette. The side image also places a hose immediately over the crown. This creates an unresolved clearance/tangency impression. These images do **not** prove that the hose penetrates the helmet.
- **Shoulder hose:** The cream hose crosses the upper chest from a large dark fitting at the nearer shoulder. The fitting obscures the shoulder/sleeve transition and visually competes with the harness. It looks like a large external plug resting against the suit; the visible images do not establish a sewn-in or armored attachment. This is a construction/readability issue, not a confirmed body intersection.
- **Near elbow and sleeve:** The side view exposes a sharp, stepped junction where the descending upper arm meets the horizontal forearm. The front-quarter view confirms a blocky corner instead of fabric folding around the bend. No open skin-colored hole is visible, but the joint contour does not convincingly describe a clothed elbow.
- **Wrists and grips:** The nearer front-quarter wrist has a large dark ring with the smaller hand emerging from it. Fingers visibly turn under the grip. There is no clearly provable detached hand or gross finger-through-grip collision in these views. The uniform fingers and limited visible thumb opposition still leave the grasp feeling manufactured rather than loaded.
- **Lap, thighs, and console:** In the lower center of the front-quarter image, angular slate-colored leg/lap forms overlap raised diagonal cockpit panels. The side image also hides much of the pelvis behind a large diagonal panel. The visible boundaries make the seated anatomy difficult to parse. Occlusion prevents a reliable claim of thigh/console penetration or correct clearance.
- **Restraint continuity:** The visible chest runs terminate visually into the dark lap/torso region without a clearly readable buckle and attachment path. This is a visible continuity gap in the restraint design. It is not evidence that unseen anchors are absent.

There is no unambiguous large mesh-through-mesh collision that can be established from these two projections alone. The stronger visible problems are primitive joint construction, ambiguous contact/clearance, weak restraint continuity, and insufficient material and medium-scale surface detail.

## Top three fixes

1. **Rebuild the clothed body and helmet at the medium scale.** Settle the helmet closer into a shaped collar, strengthen the shoulders, shape the upper arms and forearms around real elbows, and establish a clear pelvis-to-thigh seated transition. Add purposeful helmet shell breaks, a more integrated visor/face construction, suit panels, elbow compression, padded seams, and glove knuckles. Additional tiny bolts or surface noise will not resolve the current silhouette and construction gap.
2. **Make the driver, restraints, and controls read as one fitted assembly.** Show continuous shoulder webbing into a readable lap buckle and credible visible routing toward the seat. Give each palm a convincing grip diameter, differentiated finger bends, thumb opposition, and contact compression. Clarify the thigh/console boundary and separate the overhead hose from the helmet silhouette. Reduce or reroute the chest hose so the harness and driver pose remain readable.
3. **Author material separation and wear to the target's level.** Make the suit visibly textile, restraints woven webbing, gloves padded fabric/leather, helmet worn coated shell, visor glass, and controls distinct metal/rubber. Use restrained seams, abrasion, paint loss, roughness variation, and contact shading in places that explain use and construction. Reduce the dominance of the pale hoses and rods, and establish a coherent instrument/control arrangement in the currently sparse forward cockpit region.

A premium re-review should use the same two views with the wrists, lap restraint, and helmet clearance clearly visible. The current study is useful for seating and control placement, but it has a substantial modeling and finish gap to close before an 8/10 judgment would be justified.
