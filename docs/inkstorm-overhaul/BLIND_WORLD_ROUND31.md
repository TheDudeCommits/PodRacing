# Blind whole-world visual review — Round 31 final

**Strict visual parity: FAIL. Mean section score: 4.79/10. Sections passing: 0/7.**

This is an independent image-only review of the seven original world concepts and all nineteen requested candidate world views in `output/gauntlet/round-31-final/`. No source, implementation documents, other reviews, capture receipts, or prior-round images informed the judgment. Images were inspected individually at large display size. The salt image is named `02-salt-run.png` in both directories; the requested `02-salt.png` was absent.

Scores assess realization of the supplied visual target, including composition, depth, geology, material construction, vehicle presence, and cohesion. A 5 means recognizable target intent with major art gaps; an 8 means competitive realization with smaller refinements remaining. PASS requires the target's defining composition and material richness to hold in the available world views. Scores and parity are separate judgments. HUD text is not evidence of performance, speed, physics, route functionality, or playability, and none of those are evaluated here.

## World-wide art assessment

The candidate has a coherent basic palette: orange ground and sunlit rock, violet shadows, blue-gray machinery, and cyan accents. Landmarks differ enough to identify a settlement, natural arch, elevated branch, launch vista, industrial district, and fenced cliff bend. There is worthwhile spatial structure here, especially at the arch, launch, and foundry.

The cohesion currently comes more from color and recurring assets than from a shared, fully authored construction language. The concepts use broad painted value groups alongside selective hard edges, fractured horizontal strata, irregular silhouettes, dusty occlusion, warm bounce light, and concentrated mechanical detail. The candidate combines smoothly shaded large mesa walls, highly craggy repeated rock pieces, very regular pipe-and-cylinder machinery, plain geometric road fittings, and a more detailed sky. These elements do not yet share the same apparent level of finish.

The vehicle is clearly present in every supplied candidate view: two blue-gray engines, cyan rear cores, connecting cables, an open cockpit, and a visible helmeted driver. That silhouette is understandable. Relative to the targets, however, the engines are narrowly spaced, long, and smoothly enclosed; much of each hull is a quiet flat surface. The target makes the engines broad foreground anchors with large exposed rings, layered armor, orange bands, fins, plumbing, and thick cables. The candidate's cockpit and pilot occupy a smaller visual role, and the thin electric link has little impact. The higher, farther chase framing also creates substantially more empty road around the vehicle. This is a whole-world composition issue, not merely a missing close-up texture.

## Top three next macro art priorities

1. **Author a continuous geological landscape with deliberate depth layers.** Build coherent shelves, erosion channels, talus aprons, undercuts, fractured strata, and varied spire groups around the road; join them convincingly to the ground. Replace the repeated craggy skyline masses and isolated roadside rock rhythm with larger, varied geological relationships. Use foreground occlusion, intermediate formations, value separation, and dust/haze to make distant landforms recede. The salt run needs a strong long-distance sweep; the canyon needs a composed sunlit vista beyond the arch; the launch needs a basin with several visible scales of terrain. Acceptance should be judged at the approach, landmark, and departure, so one attractive hero framing cannot conceal empty connecting space.

2. **Stage each landmark around its defining spatial idea and believable construction.** Rebuild the fork around a dominant natural divider and an upper route visibly embedded in rock. Give the grid a continuous, inhabited workshop street with canopies and irregular frontages. Develop the foundry into connected working assemblies with varied tower profiles, substantial supports, platforms, bridge machinery, and convincing foundations; its current density should be retained while repeated kits become less obvious. Give the finish a strong banked wall/barrier and a framed endpoint. Material wear should follow joints, exposed edges, leaks, repairs, and human use, with metal, cloth, stone, and sand reading differently. These changes matter more than adding many small random props.

3. **Rebuild the vehicle's foreground authority and unify the final rendering language.** Establish a broad, powerful engine silhouette and a more visible pilot/cockpit relationship before adding fine detail. Break up the enclosed hulls with layered armor, exposed mechanical rings and plumbing, purposeful orange-blue color blocking, fins, cable weight, and localized wear. Tune chase-camera composition so the vehicle anchors the scene while the road and landmark remain visible. Carry the same edge treatment, shadow richness, and material response into world structures; resolve flat bright objects and stark geometric fittings that currently sit outside that language. Review this in all seven sections, including canyon shade and foundry shadow, rather than accepting only a well-lit vehicle view.

The images support recognition of the intended world and several strong landmark foundations. They do **not** support strict parity with the original concepts. The main remaining work is at the level of terrain composition, spatial staging, asset construction, and vehicle silhouette; surface noise or extra effects alone would leave those gaps intact.
