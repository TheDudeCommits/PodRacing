## Concrete regressions to avoid carrying forward

1. **Do not darken all blue categories equally.** In pit 1–2, B removes the bright outer turbine-ring read and weakens the foreground blue chest's front-to-handle separation. In district 1 and 4, wall, shelf recesses, and engine shell crowd the same value range. These are functional silhouette losses, not objections to a darker palette itself.
2. **Keep visible construction edges on shaded assemblies.** The pit hoist header and district upper crossbeam are much less distinct in B. Fine surface mottling cannot replace the large-scale indication of which member is in front of which.
3. **Do not let the distant service district become an undifferentiated strip.** In gantry 1, the main beam benefits from restraint, but the surrounding workshops lose recognizability. The focal gantry and background activity need different material/value decisions.
4. **Avoid assigning metal-like broad shading to every blue surface.** B's panel response is useful on rigid shells. On awnings it still does not communicate the concept's thin cloth, tension, folds, and sun transmission. Extra texture alone does not establish material identity.

## Strongest next improvement

**Build one readable, warmly lit service bay around the turbine, then judge it in both the shaded and illuminated pit cameras.** Retain B's restrained weathered blue, but introduce a motivated warm task-light source and soft interior bounce that separate the turbine lip, blades, hoist, workbench, and floor. Give the exposed metal lip and major frame edges selective highlights, keep the cavity behind them dark, and reserve cyan for the small active core. Restore object separation through distinct material response and local illumination rather than lifting every blue surface together.

This addresses the biggest visible weakness in the current A/B tradeoff: B has better paint treatment without enough light or material contrast to make that treatment readable in the service areas. Acceptance should require that the turbine's outer ring, inner blades, suspension frame, and nearest storage chest remain distinguishable at the existing camera distance in pit 1, pit 2, and pit 3. A warm pool should reveal believable work activity without competing with the gantry's cyan signals.

The concept's strongest lesson is selective information: mechanical focal areas are richly articulated, sunlit cloth and ground remain broader, and recesses contain warm, readable activity. Neither blanket darkening nor blanket texture density achieves that hierarchy.
