# Blind exhaust-mouth review — Round 27

**Verdict: 6.7/10 — FAIL against a strict 8/10 threshold. RETAIN B and refine it.** B is visibly better than A, but the mouths still read partly as luminous circular inserts rather than convincingly hot, recessed machinery.

This is an image-only judgment of exhaust mouths and their attachment. I viewed all six new gameplay images, both older Teemto gameplay images, and both generated vehicle targets with the image-viewing tool. World terrain and lighting quality are excluded. No source, handover, previous critique, browser, or Blender was consulted.

| Candidate | Score | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| A: older Teemto, `round27-exhaust-v1`, grid and canyon | 4.8/10 | FAIL |
| B: new Teemto, `round27-nozzle-v1`, grid, canyon, launch | 6.6/10 | FAIL |
| B: new Sebulba, `round27-nozzle-v1-sebulba`, grid, canyon, launch | 6.8/10 | FAIL |

A's near-uniform cyan face and radial pattern read like a thin fan badge at the end of each engine. B adds a materially thicker outer circumference, a darker outside edge, and a visible inner wall around the smaller luminous center. In both grid and canyon comparisons, that creates a bowl-like recess instead of A's predominantly flat disc. The small white-cyan core remains legible at gameplay scale. These are worthwhile gains and justify retaining B.

B does not yet establish the intended material hierarchy. A broad cyan inner ring remains nearly as visually insistent as the core. In grid and launch, the lower interior contains large pale beige/gray facets; these make the opening read as a clean reflective cup or mechanical eye. In canyon, the darker lower cavity improves recession, but the continuous cyan ring still outlines the opening like decoration. The outer dark band helps, yet the throat lacks a sustained heat-dark appearance leading back to the core.

Teemto's nozzle meets the pointed engine casing abruptly, with the circular collar appearing appended to its tip. I see contact rather than an obvious floating gap, but little visible transition housing that makes the connection feel engineered. Thin dark lines across the upper mouth add clutter around the core. Sebulba's thicker collar sits more convincingly within the surrounding drum; however, its clean cyan and pale interior feels visually separate from the scorched, worn surrounding metal. This is a material and transition mismatch, not a confirmed physical disconnection.

I do **not** see a definite view through a nozzle to recognizable background, nor a clearly detached lip. The pale lower sectors can give a see-through-like impression, but these stills do not establish that they are holes. I make no claim about hidden geometry, animation, or performance.

The targets support a standard of dark recessed hardware, worn metal edges, and believable transitions between assemblies. Neither target provides a directly comparable rear view of this exact cyan exhaust-mouth design, so I am not treating them as proof of a required ring pattern or core color. B approaches their sense of mechanical layering but remains substantially cleaner and more graphic around the opening.

Three fixes, in priority order:

1. **Make the throat consistently dark and clearly recessed.** Replace the broad pale lower cup appearance with a deeper charcoal/heat-stained interior, retain enough directional edge shading to reveal the inner wall, and keep the small hot core visibly behind the lip.
2. **Concentrate the cyan energy at the core.** Reduce the brightness and continuity of the full cyan ring, letting dark metal and restrained reflected cyan separate the core from the rim. Preserve the center's readability at the current gameplay size.
3. **Integrate the collar into each vehicle.** Give Teemto a visible tapered or stepped junction from casing to nozzle; carry Sebulba's worn dark metal treatment onto the collar and lip. Keep the mouth silhouette clean enough that upper crossing lines do not obscure the recess.
