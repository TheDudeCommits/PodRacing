## Pits: useful equipment, weaker fabric

- **Pit 1:** B adds a readable service story: a large radial component under a hoist, grounded hoist feet, drawer units, and substantial storage chests. A's nearly empty floor and two small glowing cylinders read closer to a blockout display. B's medium-sized objects bridge some of the scale gap between the huge shell and tiny people. However, the large blue-rimmed fan dominates the opening and partially hides the existing left engine. Adjacent hoist uprights and equipment overlap without much separation. The repeated blue/orange chest palette and clean broad faces still feel like toy props.
- **Pit 2:** The same improvement survives the brighter ground/background view. This image makes the canopy regression especially clear: A's blue sheet includes a pale repaired-looking end panel and a thin sagging edge; B presents a more uniform blue top above a deep purple band and a strongly outlined rim. The edge reads like a shaped building component. Hoist, fan, left drawer unit, and right chests retain almost exactly the same staging seen in pit 1, which makes the setting feel templated.
- **Pit 3:** B's equipment remains legible against the brighter canyon wall, so its benefit is not confined to one background. The foreground barrier hides part of the left work area in both versions; B's large central fan remains visible, while the left drawer unit is less useful from this angle. The pale edge highlights emphasize B's layered canopy thickness rather than fine fabric construction. The same central hoist/fan and right-hand chest arrangement recurs again.

**Retain:** B's service hoists, drawer units, storage, and radial component as a useful starting kit. Preserve A's thin fabric silhouette, visible color repair, and lighter-looking edge while improving tension, seams, and attachment detail. B's new equipment should become an authored repair scene rather than a repeated front-facing product display. Both versions still have enormous empty dark shell interiors, extremely simple framing, and undersized block-shaped people; adding a few foreground boxes does not resolve that hierarchy.

## Districts: small additions do not resolve the larger spatial problems

- **District 1:** B's wheeled drawer unit at the right of the main bay creates a useful silhouette and recognizable workshop function. Small suspended fixtures add another scale of detail. The central engine remains visible, and these additions do not materially obstruct the people. But the canopy underside becomes a broad purple mass with conspicuous layered edging. A's warmer patched cloth reads more distinctly from the dark shelving behind it. The upper sign is already poorly exposed in A; B does not restore its readability.
- **District 2:** A mound of sand occupies much of the central workshop floor in both images. Equipment and the lower engine stand appear partly swallowed by it. B's right-hand drawer unit is a clean local addition, but its placement outside that buried center does not make the overall workshop more plausible. Shelf contents remain sparse little wedges against very large dark panels. The silhouette of the B awning is regular and heavily trimmed, again closer to roofing than loose fabric.
- **District 3:** This is the clearest district comparison because the central platform is exposed. The B cart improves the right foreground, and the engine/bench remain unobstructed. Yet most of the central wall, shelves, floor, and left crate group are unchanged. The cart is a localized upgrade; the large dark canopy band is a much larger visual change. It removes some of the warmer material separation visible in A.
- **District 4:** A foreground dune hides the crate group, most floor detail, and lower portions of the people. B's cart is only partially visible behind it. Additional low props have limited value from this actual camera position. The canopy change remains conspicuous because it occupies the unobstructed upper part of the view. This pair most strongly demonstrates why more props alone will not fix the district.

**Retain:** B's recognizable mobile storage and small fixtures, subject to placement variation. Use A's thinner, warmer fabric reading as the basis for the next cloth pass. Across the district views, the same front-facing central engine, paired side storage, near-identical shelf panels, crate row, and people cluster repeat. The images do not demonstrate enough bay-specific activities or distinct silhouettes. Both versions also have long nearly featureless blue walls and poles that contribute more regular lines than convincing service complexity.

## Gantries: retain B, but the gain is small

- **Gantry 1:** B preserves the primary portal silhouette, open triangular span, visible five-signal group, and clear central passage. Fine horizontal members and two small off-center suspended fixtures add believable secondary construction without competing with the signals. The background workshops also visibly gain equipment and altered canopies in B, so the overall increase in visible detail cannot be attributed to the gantry alone. Repeated workshop kits remain apparent at this wider distance.
- **Gantry 2:** B's small additions survive a different industrial backdrop. The upper span has more secondary lines, and the two extra hanging fixtures give its underside modest depth. They do not crowd the central lights or reduce passage readability. The large upright posts, repeated flat triangular braces, and blue connection strips remain very simple compared with the target's layered, worn machinery. Neither version gains much material separation here.

**Retain B.** I see no meaningful primary silhouette or passage-occlusion regression in either gantry pair. Keep the readable signal hierarchy. Further work should concentrate on a few visible joints, housings, cable routes, and structural depth; a uniform row of more hanging items would weaken the present clarity.

## Highest-impact next steps

1. **Restore fabric as fabric.** Reduce the thick fascia-like edge and broad uniform dark band. Introduce thin hems, visible corner attachments, tension toward those attachments, irregular folds, and a few restrained repaired panels. Maintain distinct blue/orange cloth identities even on the underside. Check pits 2/3 and districts 2/3 first, where the rigid-roof reading is unambiguous.
2. **Resolve occupied-floor burial and view obstruction.** Districts 2 and 4 show sand covering work areas, engine supports, people, and new equipment. Make the relationship between dune, threshold, usable platform, and service circulation plausible. Re-review from these same views; a prop hidden behind the dune does not supply useful detail.
3. **Author distinct work activities and silhouettes.** Use the B equipment kit asymmetrically: vary hoist placement, engine orientation, open versus closed storage, repair state, and clusters of people/tools. Avoid repeating the large central fan, left cart, right chests, and identical adjacent bay at every location. Preserve empty working space where it explains circulation.
4. **Build the missing middle scale inside workshops.** Add convincing shell ribs, localized benches, stacked parts, attached hoses/cables, connections, and material transitions around the actual repair activity. Give the interior enough depth and readable local value differences that it stops being a dark curved backdrop behind a few bright props. Differentiate cloth, painted metal, bare metal, and dusty equipment rather than applying the same flat blue/orange vocabulary to each.
5. **Concentrate gantry detail at functional locations.** Develop brace joints, plate depth, a small number of asymmetric utility housings, and supported cable paths while retaining B's clear open span and central signal group. The gantry already has a better large-scale construction hierarchy than the workshops; it is lower priority than the cloth and floor issues.
