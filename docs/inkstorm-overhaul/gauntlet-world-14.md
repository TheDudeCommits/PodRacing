## Shared foreground machinery

The twin engines and flexible cockpit cables form a readable, connected vehicle. Rings, straps, exposed cyan elements, and small surface chips create a consistent mechanical rhythm; this is more authored than an unadorned pair of cylinders. The garage view also makes the vehicle classes visually distinct at thumbnail scale.

The reference's machinery is substantially broader, lower, and more layered. Its asymmetric panel breaks, substantial fins, recessed turbine interiors, secondary pipework, cockpit shroud, and body-scale wear make it feel assembled and heavy. The actual engines are dominated by repeated cylindrical bands and bright flat cyan rings. The cockpit is an open, boxy cavity, the pilot appears as a small faceted head and angular body, and the cables repeat thick rings with highly uniform spacing. Surface treatment shifts between brush-like marks, pixel-like square patches, and clean flat faces, so the vehicle and environment do not yet share one convincing painterly material language.

The engine framing sits low and relatively narrow within the actual image, while the reference uses broad lateral machinery to anchor the entire foreground. This is partly a different image aspect ratio, so exact pixel matching would be inappropriate. Even allowing for that, the missing fins, simplified pilot/cockpit, and lack of metal depth remain visible design gaps. The electrical link is a bright repeated ribbon shape rather than the concept's branching luminous discharge. Its visual integration needs work; motion behavior cannot be judged here.

## Highest-impact improvements

1. **Restore the defining composition of each location.** Make the fork visibly split around a major central mass; reveal the basin and descending route at launch; extend the salt-run view into several receding landscape layers. Fill the grid's sides with a dense but legible garage district. Build a substantial retaining structure and cliff enclosure at the finish. These changes matter more than another palette adjustment.
2. **Replace repeated rock silhouettes with authored geological masses.** Use broad stratified cliffs, shelves, undercuts, fractured ledges, distinct large landmarks, and clustered rubble at their feet. Vary large, medium, and small forms while preserving the road's visual clarity. The present pointed fins and simplified boulders recur too visibly across settings.
3. **Create a consistent lighting and depth hierarchy.** Give near objects stronger contact and occlusion, let midground forms overlap, and reduce distant contrast through warm dusty atmosphere. Compose larger coherent shadow areas, especially under the canyon arch and industrial pipework, with restrained bright rim planes. The current sky has more painterly richness than the geometry beneath it.
4. **Finish one hero vehicle at the same material standard as the world.** Refine the cockpit shell and pilot, introduce substantial fins and layered panel edges, deepen turbine cavities, vary exposed mechanical detail, and make wear follow construction and use. Replace square patch noise and overly even band repetition with deliberate brush-scale marks and localized chips. Keep cyan emission supported by dark cavities and nearby reflected light.
5. **Integrate industrial construction into the terrain.** Add foundations, access platforms, connected overhead networks, supported pipe transitions, weathered joints, cables, equipment clusters, and settled debris. Reduce identical tank repetition and unexplained dark platform strips. The foundry needs credible assemblies at multiple scales, not merely more cylinders.
6. **Unify effects with the material language.** The yellow outlined overlap in the fork and pale angular shapes near the finish are conspicuous outliers. Give dust and energy controlled silhouettes, depth, color response, and occlusion so they belong to the same scene. Confirm their behavior separately in motion; these stills alone do not determine whether they are transient artifacts.

The next art review should use the same seven matching views after the composition and construction changes. A higher score should come from visible depth, authored geometry, credible machinery, and finished materials rather than the presence of more objects or matching orange and cyan colors.
