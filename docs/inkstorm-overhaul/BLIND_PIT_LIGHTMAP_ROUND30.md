## Top three actionable changes

1. **Make each bay's warm work light visibly shape the scene.** In both `pit-1` images, the left inner arch and bench remain navy-purple where Target 2 has a concentrated amber patch, bright working edge, and falling light across nearby surfaces. In both `pit-2` images, the right rear panel remains a dull blue/gray stripe behind the engine where the target has a warm illuminated work zone. Create a clear bright-source-to-surface relationship at those two workstations, preserve dark upper recesses, and add tight contact darkening under case feet, engine stands, and wall-mounted pieces. Judge success by whether a viewer immediately identifies two illuminated work areas without looking at the lamp heads.

2. **Turn the prop arrangement into a legible repair operation with layered silhouettes and credible crew scale.** The left fan in every candidate is dominated by flat radial sectors, and the right engine remains a short smooth cylinder. Add stepped rims, separate blades or vents, fastening bands, hoses, hoist connections, and overlapping tools that survive this camera distance. Place a readable mechanic at the left bench and another interacting with the right engine, with differentiated limbs and task poses. Target 2 makes the left worker approximately bench-to-head height and gives the right worker clear contact with the machine; the candidates' small vertical forms do not convey that relationship. Preserve clean gaps around those silhouettes so the lift posts do not hide them.

3. **Separate painted metal, bare machinery, cloth, and worn floor through visible finish and construction detail.** The broad left arch in `pit-1`/`pit-2` currently contributes a large nearly featureless dark region, while Target 2 articulates it with shell ribs, seams, pipes, worn edges, and irregular warm reflection. Add those medium-scale structures and finish differences first. The central blue case in `pit-3` is sufficiently lit to expose the gap: its mostly clean rectangular faces and simple edging need distinct chipped paint, metal hardware, recessed seams, and grounded feet. Give canopy edges reinforced seams and more convincing sag/tension, and vary floor wear around the actual work areas rather than relying on broad noise. The result should retain distinct material identities when viewed without relying on their base colors.

A fresh comparison is warranted only after these changes become visible in the actual candidate images.
